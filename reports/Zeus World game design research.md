# Zeus World succeeds as a hosted show

Zeus World cannot become "the next GTA" by matching GTA's content, and it does not need to. What transfers from GTA to a world people watch is its structure: a small, dense, hand-made place, visible everyday life, occasional surprises and a strong voice. The precedents for audience-driven worlds give a blunt second lesson: the input gimmick fades in days, and the projects that lasted gave people a character, a story or a personal stake to come back to, with a human visibly running the show. The existing constitution (free belonging, no spend rankings, nothing erased, absence never punished, narrator limited to logged facts) lines up closely with what the evidence rewards and with what regulators and audiences have punished elsewhere. The biggest risks sit outside the game design. TikTok's monetisation rules penalise streams that run mainly on automation without a present host, that repeatedly prompt for gifts, or that involve chance. There is also no official TikTok data feed for LIVE events, so the whole project rests on an unofficial connection that can break without notice. AI narration is now cheap, roughly one to three US dollars per five-hour stream on the notes' estimates, and its remaining problems are safety and rhythm. Two of the owner's requests need care: a battle "MVP" as TikTok defines it is in effect the top gifter, which collides with the constitution, and "damage after a lost battle" has to be temporary and repairable for free.

A note on how to read this report. "Well evidenced" means a peer-reviewed study, a developer's own account or a regulator's action. "Case evidence" means it happened in one or a few named projects. "Inference" means the reasoning is the researchers' or mine and no source tested it. TikTok's own policy pages could not be opened directly during the research; their wording here comes from search extracts and third-party guides, so the owner should read the linked official pages before relying on exact wording.

## GTA's structure transfers to a watched world; its controller feel does not

### What Rockstar says makes its worlds work

Rockstar's own account is "place first, plot second". Its art director describes starting from a map and filling it with interesting locations, with missions added afterwards ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=80936), seen in summary only). The team deliberately **condensed** Los Angeles into a space players could cross comfortably, aiming for "the essence of what's really there in a city, but in a far smaller area", filled the countryside with wildlife so it would not feel "hollow", added optional random events the player can take or ignore, and treated audio as a pillar: 241 licensed tracks across fifteen music stations plus two talk stations and over twenty hours of score ([Wikipedia, Development of GTA V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)). None of those four things (a dense place, ambient life, optional surprises, a voice) needs anyone to hold a controller.

The scale does not transfer. GTA V took a core team of 360, over 1,000 people in total, and an estimated development budget above US$137 million ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)). Red Dead Redemption 2 has about 500,000 lines of dialogue and 300,000 animations ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/red-dead-redemption-2-has-a-60-hour-story-500000-lines-of-dialogue)). For one person with AI help, "the next GTA" can only mean GTA's structure. The research found no measured study of why GTA is replayable; this section rests on what developers said in interviews.

### The better model is the story generator

The most useful theory for Zeus World comes from Tynan Sylvester, the designer of RimWorld, a game built by a tiny team and described by its studio "not as a game, but as a story generator" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024686/-RimWorld-Contrarian-Ridiculous-and)). His argument is that a simulation's value lies entirely in the picture it puts in the observer's head: anything the simulation computes that the observer cannot perceive "is worthless". People naturally see intention and drama in simple events, so the simulation "does the logistics" and the human "adds the meaning". He also names a cheap trick: details that affect nothing else in the system, such as a character's appearance or history, add flavour at almost no cost. And he warns that complexity backfires: in a village of a hundred where everyone has a relationship with everyone, nobody can follow any of it ([Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)).

This is an argument about what an observer perceives, so it applies to viewers as much as players (inference, but a direct one). Three consequences follow for Zeus World. First, **simple and readable beats deep**: 58 citizens with visible trades, a daily routine and a quirk each will generate more story than a hidden economy. Second, **cast size matters**: RimWorld keeps to around a dozen named characters, and a competitive game built for watching, Amazon's Breakaway, cut its teams from 24 a side to 4 a side so viewers could follow it ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/building-games-that-are-fun-to-watch-fun-to-play-in-the-twitch-era)). A city of many citizens needs a small foreground cast at any moment, with the rest as crowd. Third, the creator of Dwarf Fortress observes that the stories people cherish come from things that mechanically happened, and that machine-written text has not yet produced a story people love ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/game-development/dwarf-fortress-creator-says-we-have-a-natural-lean-towards-human-made-stories-but-but-were-all-going-to-be-very-sorely-tested-in-the-coming-years-by-ai/), summary only). That supports the constitution's rule that the narrator may only report what the event log contains.

### Cheap techniques that make a place feel alive

The documented techniques arrange attention; they do not add content. The Witcher 3 team required something of interest roughly every **40 seconds** of travel ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/59420/witcher-3s-40-second-rule-kept-players-engaged/index.html), summary only), and one of its designers later regretted the filler added just to meet that quota ([GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/6828-the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-dev-says-he-went-overboard-points-of-interest-on-the-map), summary only). Nintendo found that evenly spaced attractions made Breath of the Wild feel like a guided line; placing landmarks of different size and importance pulled players in different directions, and shapes that half-hide what is behind them created a chain of reveals ([translation of Nintendo's CEDEC 2017 talks](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399)). Red Dead Redemption 2 scripts ordinary inhabitants from waking to work to the saloon to bed, and fans made videos simply following one of them for a day ([Business Insider](https://businessinsider.nl/red-dead-redemption-2-following-npcs-for-full-day-video-2018-11), summary only).

For a watched city these translate, by inference, into camera rules: within any stretch of about 40 seconds the frame should contain something that changes; several things of different size should be happening at once so chat has something to point at; the camera tour should reveal landmarks from behind other things; and a "follow this citizen" shot is a proven pleasure. The owner's request for people working, markets, celebrations and dancing is exactly this layer. It is cheap, and the evidence supports it more strongly than anything else in this report.

### What does and does not carry over

The table below is a judgement built on the findings above; no source rates these qualities directly.

| Quality of GTA-style worlds | Transfers to a watched, collectively steered world? |
| --- | --- |
| The feel of driving, shooting, moving | No. This is the largest part of GTA's appeal that cannot be carried over. |
| Personal freedom to go anywhere | Weakly. Replace it with a personal stake (own citizen, own home) plus collective steering. |
| Mischief and causing chaos | Risky. In a shared city it becomes vandalising other people's property. |
| Dense, condensed, hand-made place | Yes, strongly. |
| Ambient life and daily routines | Yes, strongly. Viewers have time to notice what players rush past. |
| Stories emerging from simple systems | Yes, strongly. |
| Random events and set pieces | Yes. They become the shared "did you see that" moments. |
| Radio, tone, voice | Yes. The narrator, music and crowd sound carry this. |
| Persistence and ownership | Yes, and more strongly, because the stake is social. |

## Every precedent spiked, and only shows with a host, a story or a stake lasted

### The novelty curve

Almost every audience-driven experiment shows the same curve: a spike measured in days, then a steep fall. Twitch Plays Pokemon drew 1.16 million participants and a peak of about 121,000 concurrent viewers in sixteen days in 2014, and at least nine in ten of its viewers were watching, not typing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_Plays_Pok%C3%A9mon)). The identical mechanic applied to the next game drew about 20,000 viewers within days ([Latin Post](https://www.latinpost.com/articles/8391/20140305/twitch-plays-pokemon-live-stream-update-crystal-version-social-experiment.htm), summary only). It also showed the tension between chaos and control: pure free-for-all made progress impossible, and switching to voting made part of the community revolt, because the chaos had been generating their shared story ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_Plays_Pok%C3%A9mon)). "Nothing, Forever", an endless AI-generated sitcom, went from about four average viewers to over ten thousand on press attention, was suspended for fourteen days after an offensive generated joke, and collapsed to very low numbers afterwards ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/thousands-of-people-cant-stop-watching-ai-tv-show-nothing-forever/); [TwitchTracker](https://twitchtracker.com/watchmeforever)). It was later reported stuck for days with characters walking into a fridge before anyone fixed it ([inkl](https://inkl.com/news/ai-driven-perpetual-seinfeld-falls-into-further-ruin-its-puppets-stuck-walking-into-a-fridge-for-at-least-five-days-before-anyone-fixed-it), summary only). Reddit's r/place drew enormous participation three times, yet its 2023 edition did not grow during the event as earlier ones had, turned into a protest against its own host, and has not run since ([arXiv 2408.13236](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13236v2)).

### What the survivors had

**Rival Peak** is the closest precedent to Zeus World and the only one found whose audience grew week after week: a 24-hour simulated world of twelve AI characters on Facebook, steered by viewers over twelve weeks, with weekly minutes watched ending at over 55 times the first week's level by the company's own figures ([Genvid](https://genvid.com/?p=4224)). Its makers credit fixes shipped during the run: recap videos inside the stream, short written story recaps, an achievements page so viewers could see their own involvement, pooled community goals that unlocked a scene, mini-games to fill the wait of up to 90 seconds between a vote and its result, a tutorial for newcomers, and a weekly human-hosted recap show that became "its own lore generator" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-real-time-evolution-of-i-rival-peak-i-)). They also describe it as needing round-the-clock staffing in four regions, which a one-person team cannot copy. The transferable part is the recaps, the collective goals and the host.

The other survivors make the same point from different directions. Neuro-sama, an AI character, became the most-subscribed channel on Twitch in January 2026 with over 160,000 subscribers ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/05/neuro-sama-vedal987-most-subscribed-hype-train-record)), with her human developer visibly present alongside her. The Dream SMP, a Minecraft story that viewers only watched, peaked at 650,237 concurrent viewers ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com:443/world-records/657589-most-viewers-of-a-minecraft-gameplay-livestream-on-twitch)); when the server closed, the final act was a visit to the still-standing house of a member who had died ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=173299), summary only). Marbles on Stream shows a humbler success: a simple loop a streamer can run nightly, flat at around 166 concurrent streamers through 2026 ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1170970)). It never spikes and never dies.

### The most punished mistake: selling influence

Silent Hill: Ascension, from the same company as Rival Peak, weighted votes by points that could be bought, capped what free viewers could earn, launched its chat unmoderated and had to switch it off, and opened decisive votes only during a US-evening broadcast. Reception was hostile from the first week, the chief executive conceded mistakes publicly, and the service was later shut down ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/under-the-microscope-heres-whats-wrong-with-silent-hill-ascension/); [The Cutting Room Floor](https://tcrf.net/Silent_Hill:_Ascension)). No source measured how many viewers left because of pay-weighted voting, so this is case evidence, but it is consistent: no creator in the research credited paid influence as a success. Selling spectacle, by contrast, draws complaints about spending without the same community revolt (inference from the cases). The constitution's "collective over individual" and "no rankings by spend" are on the right side of this.

### Named viewers and owned property

The evidence that a named in-world identity brings people back is suggestive but unmeasured. Cult of the Lamb let viewers become named followers in a streamer's game, sold a million copies in a week, and its developers later added a gallery where viewers can look up their follower's level and traits ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2022/08/19/twitch-integration-streaming-cult-of-the-lamb-interactive/); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034489/Independent-Games-Summit-Cult-of)). Rival Peak added achievements because viewers asked to gauge their own involvement. In r/place, small groups formed around an identity defended their patch for days, and smaller groups had proportionally more active members than huge ones ([arXiv 2408.13236](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13236v2)). When a streamer overrode a choice his chat had made in Choice Chamber, the chat punished him until he promised not to repeat it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/twitch-plays-everything-designing-for-a-new-frontier)). Identity that was earned or freely given was welcomed; identity sold as a paid perk in Ascension was resented. No study isolates the effect on return visits, so treat "homes with name plates bring people back" as a well-supported belief, not a proven fact. One caution for the streamer-override rule: use it openly and rarely, because audiences treat their contributions as owned.

### What research on audience participation adds

A Carnegie Mellon study of audience participation games is the best academic source, and it speaks directly to Zeus World's design. Giving each viewer individual control breaks down with large audiences; the authors recommend "social agency", where collaboration leads to collective power and collective rewards. **Delay destroys the sense of impact**: viewers saw the effect of their input 6 to more than 20 seconds late, and with votes tallied every 30 seconds they could not tell whether their vote counted. Voting alone did not build community; chat lacks identity markers such as avatars and levels, so the bonds "must come from game design". Bored participants became trolls, and repeating the same command was what bored them. Mechanics "that show clear evidence of an individual's contribution" hold interest, and good audience effects give the streamer "performable moments" to react to ([Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)). The study's sessions were single one-hour sittings with a handful of participants; nobody has studied a persistent audience-built world over weeks, and all of this research is from Twitch, not TikTok.

For Zeus World this means effects should be slow, lasting and labelled with the contributor's name, never fast and fleeting. A building that carries the names of those who raised it fits. TikTok LIVE's actual delay was not found in any source and must be measured on a real stream. Practitioner advice on watchability adds that a first-time viewer must grasp what is happening within seconds and that rare, outcome-changing moments should be highlighted with replays ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/building-games-that-are-fun-to-watch-fun-to-play-in-the-twitch-era)). That matters more on TikTok, where viewers arrive cold mid-stream all the time (inference): a permanent on-screen line saying what the city is building now and what just happened is the equivalent of a scoreboard.

## Collective goals and permanent status retain people; streaks and spend rankings carry costs

### What motivation research supports

The best-evidenced theory of why games hold people says they satisfy three needs: a sense of choice, a sense of competence and a sense of connection ([Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski 2006](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs11031-006-9051-8.pdf)). A large survey of online game players found that achievement, social and immersion motives are separate and can all be high in the same person, so designing for "types" of viewer is weaker than giving everyone several kinds of thing to do ([Yee 2006](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/239)). A shared city-building goal serves connection well, but by itself it does not make a viewer feel competent or free to choose. The bridge is a visible, named contribution and a real choice about how to take part (inference). No study directly compares collective goals with individual goals for retention; "shared goals retain better" is supported by cases only.

Two effects from consumer research are solid and apply directly to buildings that rise with a Bolts progress label. People speed up as they near a goal, and they persist more when given a head start: in a car-wash loyalty experiment, **34% completed a card that started with two free stamps, against 19% for a plain card requiring the same effort** ([Wharton summary of Nunes and Drèze 2006](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-lowdown-on-customer-loyalty-programs-which-are-the-most-effective-and-why/)). A progress bar should therefore never start at zero, provided the head start is honest and themed ("the founders laid the first stones"), and a slow middle followed by a late surge is normal, not failure. Pokémon GO's 2017 global challenge shows the tiered version: one worldwide target, three milestones that each unlocked a bonus for everyone, and a final reward ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2017/11/26/pokemon-go-new-event-asks-players-to-catch-3-billion-pokemon-in-a-week-)). This is the model for the owner's "city levels that unlock things".

Targets must scale with the audience. World of Warcraft's famous server-wide war effort took the fastest servers 19 days in 2006 and one server 14 hours in the 2020 rerun, for the same target ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011/04/19/wow-archivist-the-gates-of-ahnqiraj/); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/world-of-warcraft-recreates-one-of-its-biggest-events-including-broken-servers/)). The same event is also the standard example of a one-time spectacle most players missed, because the gates could open in the middle of the night ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2016/04/25/wow-gates-ahnqiraj-cthun/)). Fortnite shows the cure: its Travis Scott event reached 12.3 million at the first showing and **27.7 million unique players across five showings** ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/fortnite-travis-scott-event-sees-123-million-players-in-first-show)). Showing a big moment more than once more than doubles its reach.

### A human game master beats a calendar

Helldivers 2 employs a named staff member, "Joel", as a game master who sets community-wide objectives and responds to what players do, specifically so the game does not become "a calendar with automated events tied to major holidays" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2-uses-its-game-master-to-stop-its-live-service-side-from-being-just-a-calendar-with-automated-events-tied-to-major-holidays)). Players adopted the fiction and tell the story themselves. The documented problems are suspicion of rigging in both directions and progress rules players could not see ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/the-helldivers-complete-another-major-order-by-the-skin-of-their-teeth-and-some-cant-help-but-feel-joels-going-easy-on-them)). In Zeus World the streamer already is the game master, which is an advantage a studio has to pay for. The lessons are to publish how progress is counted, to be open when the streamer puts a thumb on the scale, and to keep a visible record of what the community chose (inference from the coverage).

The opposite model, a fixed seasonal treadmill, burned out even a large studio. Bungie's director named fear of missing out from expiring content as a problem to fix in 2020, acknowledged player burnout in 2022, and the studio later conceded its structure had become predictable ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/destiny-2-lead-says-theyre-trying-to-fix-the-fomo-probl-1841936370); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/destiny-2-game-director-addresses-seasonal-burnout-your-words-are-not-falling-on-deaf-ears)). A one-person project should plan a few large, repeatable moments built from systems, not a stream of new hand-made content. No public evidence was found that one-time events raise long-term retention; peak attendance is not retention.

### Status without a bottom of the table

Research on leaderboards is mixed but consistently finds that public rankings of everyone risk discouraging those near the bottom ([Internet Research review, 2023](https://www.emerald.com/intr/article/33/7/1/178330); [NSF-hosted paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10510520)). A spend ranking is that kind of leaderboard on the one dimension most viewers cannot compete on, so the constitution's ban matches the evidence. Status that is not rank-ordered, such as a named home, a role title, a name carved on a building, or "citizen since day 12", gives the status motive an outlet with no losers. That is an inference; no study of housing or titles as retention tools was found.

Streaks are the one well-evidenced retention tool that conflicts with "absence is never punished". People continue a behaviour more when recent repetitions are logged and shown to them ([INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/marketing/consumer-streaks-are-motivating-key-keeping-them-alive)), but the mechanism in streak designs is fear of loss. A running total ("days visited: 41") keeps the logging benefit without a penalty for a missed day (inference). Animal Crossing: New Horizons dropped the series' earlier habit of punishing absence with weeds ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/what-happens-when-you-abandon-your-animal-crossing-new-1844574997)), yet Nintendo was still criticised for an email in which villagers appeared to guilt lapsed players ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/nintendo-animal-crossing-new-horizons-hiatus-guilt-trip-email/)). Punishing absence can creep back in through the host's words and the narrator's lines, so the rule has to cover those too. No data shows that gentle treatment of absence retains better than punishing treatment; this remains a values-led choice.

### The ethical exposure is the gifting layer

Regulators have acted against exactly the patterns the constitution avoids: US$245 million in refunds from Epic for purchase flows that tricked players ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/fortnite-maker-epic-games-to-settle-ftc-case-pay-520-million-in-penalties-and-refunds)), US$20 million from the maker of Genshin Impact over loot-box odds and sales to children ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)), and the permanent withdrawal in the EU of TikTok's own scheme that paid points for watching ([European Commission](https://luxembourg.representation.ec.europa.eu/actualites-et-evenements/actualites/tiktok-commits-permanently-withdraw-tiktok-lite-rewards-programme-eu-comply-digital-services-act-2024-08-05_en)). The last one bears on Bolts: rewarding acts of participation is easier to defend than rewarding minutes watched.

Research on live-stream gifting finds the viewer's one-sided bond with the host is the main driver of spending, and that viewers also gift to help a host win a competition ([NJIT record of CHI 2018 study](https://researchwith.njit.edu/en/publications/explaining-viewers-emotional-instrumental-and-financial-support-p/); [Qiu and Klug 2021](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/view/12194)). Australian research reports that up to 80% of gifts arrive during battles ([ADM+S Centre](https://www.admscentre.org.au/research-reveals-the-real-cost-of-tiktok-superstar-economy/), summary only). TikTok LIVE gifting and minors is the subject of several US state lawsuits; these are allegations, and TikTok disputes them ([Route Fifty](https://route-fifty.com/digital-government/2025/01/new-court-records-claim-tiktok-knew-its-live-feature-was-used-groom-children/401956)). So the largest remaining risk sits in the gifting layer around the game. Any moment where the city or the host reacts more warmly to gifts than to free actions, or where a collective bar is about to fall short and only gifts can close it, recreates battle pressure without any leaderboard (inference). Under-18s can watch and chat but cannot send gifts, so the design should not single out gifters in ways that invite young viewers to chase the same attention.

## AI should narrate and pace the city, while plain rules run the citizens

### Fully AI-driven citizens are neither needed nor noticed

Stanford's "Smallville" experiment in 2023 gave 25 characters memory, reflection and planning through a language model. It produced believable behaviour and cost "thousands of dollars" for two simulated days ([Park et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)), later estimated at about **US$25 per character per hour** ([Lyfe Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02172v1)). The characters also invented details, forgot things they had been told and walked into closed shops. Every follow-up cut cost the same way: call the AI rarely, let cheap rules move the bodies, and reuse earlier decisions. One follow-up found that characters in a fixed town "can only generate finite behaviors" and eventually stop producing anything new, so their decisions can be stored and reused ([Affordable Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)). A larger experiment found that mistakes compound and spread between characters, and that what a character says drifts away from what it does ([Project Sid](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)).

The commercial record agrees. inZOI shipped AI-driven characters in 2025 and reviewers could not tell them from ordinary game logic; one headline said it "kinda just looks like regular NPC AI" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/the-sims-rival-inzoi-uses-special-nvidia-ai-tech-to-power-its-characters-but-this-kinda-just-looks-like-regular-npc-ai-to-me)). The part people noticed was the visible thought bubble. The lesson for a stream is to spend AI only on what viewers can read or hear (inference).

The classic techniques do the rest for free. In The Sims each character has a few need meters, objects "advertise" which needs they satisfy, and the character picks among good options with a little randomness so it does not look robotic; putting the behaviour on the objects let the team add hundreds of objects without touching the core logic ([Game Maker's Toolkit transcript](https://amara.org/videos/cVoJS4OdmVql/en/4330642)). "Storylets" are small pieces of story with conditions for when they may play and effects on the world; they are described as "simple, atomic, robust, and recombinable" and suited to games that grow for years ([Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)). Both are deterministic, which fits the constitution and makes every narrated fact checkable.

### A director is a small, proven piece of code

Left 4 Dead's "AI Director" is a simple loop over one number, an estimate of how tense things are. It builds tension to a peak, holds it for a few seconds, then forces a quiet period of 30 to 45 seconds before building again. Its designer's stated lesson: the algorithm "adjusts pacing, not difficulty", changing how often peaks arrive and not how big they are, and big set-piece encounters are exempt from the pacing ([Mike Booth, Valve](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). RimWorld offers three storyteller personalities, a rising curve, a gentle one and pure chance, and eases off after the player has suffered a loss ([RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_Storytellers)). Caves of Qud decides that an event will happen and then finds a reason for it in existing history ([Grinblat and Bucklew 2017](https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)).

For a stream that runs many hours, the transfer is direct (inference): track an "audience energy" number that rises with big events and bursts of chat and falls over time; after each peak, schedule only small warm events for a few minutes; keep fixed set pieces, such as a festival on the hour, that happen regardless. The right timings for a town stream are unknown and must be found by watching real streams.

### Narration is affordable; the remaining problems are safety and rhythm

On official price lists read on 22 November 2026, the notes estimate narrator text for a five-hour stream (one line every 20 seconds) at roughly US$0.10 to $0.30 on the cheapest models and about $1.70 on a mid-tier model with caching, and about $0.15 for an occasional "thought" for each of 50 citizens ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); [Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). **Turning the text into a voice costs several times more than writing it**, roughly $1 to $2 per stream. A low-cost setup is on the order of **$1.50 to $3 per five-hour stream** and a premium one $5 to $10. These totals are the researchers' arithmetic from assumed call rates, not published figures; two of the listed prices are scheduled to double on 1 January 2027; real usage should be measured before budgeting.

Safety is the main risk. Both documented bans of AI streams were two weeks long and both came from offensive output slipping past a single filter. In the "Nothing, Forever" case the cause was a fallback to a weaker model on which moderation did not work ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/06/ai-generated-seinfeld-suspended-on-twitch-for-ai-generated-transphobic-jokes/)). Neuro-sama's developer says many filters and a human moderator are still needed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuro-sama)). Open-source AI-streamer tools pass viewer usernames straight into the AI's instructions ([twitch-chatgpt](https://github.com/pedrojlazevedo/twitch-chatgpt)). No incident of an attack through a username was found, but the route is plain. The notes recommend a set of safeguards, which are standard practice assembled by inference. Code, not the AI, chooses which logged events are worth narrating. The AI receives only those events and adds voice. Viewer names are inserted by code after the line is written, never fed to the AI. Any line that names something absent from the log is dropped. A second, independent filter checks output. When anything fails, the system falls back to pre-written lines, never to a weaker AI. Viewer handles shown on name plates need their own cleaning and blocklist. A watchdog for stuck states is also a requirement, given the fridge incident.

Rhythm is the other problem. A 2026 study found that systems which write a line, voice it, and only then start the next leave an average silence of 9.6 seconds; preparing lines ahead while the previous one plays cut that to 0.3 seconds, and 120 players rated the rhythm significantly better ([Kawamatsu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13322)). Keeping two or three lines ready also hides the time the safety checks take.

## TikTok's rules make a present host mandatory and the data feed unofficial

### What a LIVE Match is, as far as could be confirmed

TikTok describes a LIVE Match as a co-host mode in which creators or teams compete on points, and the host can choose whether all gifts or only specific gifts count ([TikTok Help](https://support.tiktok.com/nl/live-gifts-wallet/tiktok-live/live-match), via search extract). Viewers hold power-ups: a "boosting glove" gives up to a 30% chance of five-times points on each gift for 30 seconds, and "magic mist" hides one side's score from the other for 30 seconds; power-ups are handed out to the **top three contributors** of the winning side ([TikTok LIVE Creator Hub](https://www.tiktok.com/live/creators/en-MY/article/introduction-to-pk-live-match?name=Beginner), via search extract). Round length (commonly reported as five minutes), whether likes count, and how MVP and win streaks work could not be confirmed from an official source; a vendor notes TikTok can change these at any time ([PKTOK](https://pktok.com/guides/pk-battle-multipliers/)). The siege should therefore read the state of the battle from live data and never hard-code the rules, and it needs a designed "fog" look for the mist period when one side's score is hidden (inference).

### There is no official feed

No official TikTok service for receiving LIVE chat, gift or battle events was found. Every interactive game tool located reads the public LIVE room through a reverse-engineered connection whose own documentation calls it unofficial, "not a production-ready API", and liable to break without notice ([TikTok-Live-Connector](https://unpkg.com/tiktok-live-connector@2.4.4/README.md)). That route does deliver nearly everything Zeus World wants: chat with the viewer's handle, gifts with repeat counts, likes, shares, follows, joins, viewer counts, and battle messages including each side's totals and the end of the "punishment" phase. It depends on a third-party signing service whose free tier allows 25 simultaneous connections and 1,500 requests a day, with paid plans from US$50 a month ([Euler Stream](https://www.eulerstream.com/pricing)). Reading the public room does not require the streamer's password. Posting into chat does require handing the account's login session to a third party, which is the riskiest part and is avoidable. No report of a ban for merely reading the feed was found; that is an absence of reports, not permission. TikTok's terms on automated access were not read. The practical conclusion is that the city must keep running sensibly when the feed drops, and the project should accept that this foundation is outside its control.

For linking a viewer to a home, TikTok does offer an official sign-in for outside websites that returns a display name and avatar, subject to app review ([TikTok for Developers](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/scopes-overview)). It does not obviously return the handle seen in chat, so a typed code in chat remains the simplest link, with official sign-in as a later addition (inference).

### The rules that shape the design

TikTok's LIVE Monetization Guidelines, as extracted, remove earning from content that "mechanically and repeatedly solicits viewers to increase Gifts or engagement metrics" and from content that runs "primarily through automated systems, such as AI or other third-party tools, without meaningful creator presence, interaction, or facilitation of the LIVE experience" ([TikTok LIVE Creator Hub](https://www.tiktok.com/live/creators/en-US/rules_and_guidance/live_monetization_guidelines)). This is the central design risk. **A city that plays itself with gift prompts on screen is the pattern TikTok's automated moderation restricts, and a present, talking host is the stated remedy.** The AI narrator can be a co-host; it cannot be the host. Third-party guides add that enforcement got stricter in 2026, that unattended and looping streams now end early, and that restrictions escalate from minutes to permanent loss of gifting ([Streamer.Guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-rules); [TikFinity blog](https://blog.tikfinity.com/tiktok-live-rules/)).

Several specific rules bear on current plans. Asking for gifts is allowed, but tying gifts to a "transaction-like exchange" can become a violation ([Streamer.Guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-rules)); gifts converting to Bolts for the gifter sits close to that line, and the safer framing is that gifts trigger shared events while chat, taps and shares also earn (inference; no source draws the line precisely). Raffles and games of chance are prohibited on LIVE, including gift-to-spin formats ([Streamer.Guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-rules)), so nothing a gift produces should be random, and honours such as palace queens should not be drawn by lot. "Setting two sides against each other to be decided by gifts" is a named gift-baiting pattern ([TikFinity blog](https://blog.tikfinity.com/tiktok-live-rules/)); inside a real LIVE Match that is TikTok's own format, so the siege should exist only as a picture of a real Match. A game vendor reports that QR codes and information from other platforms are not allowed on screen, that a faceless stream may be read as a recording, and that streamers of interactive games receive automated restrictions with appeals denied instantly and no verified fix ([Livecade](https://livecade.io/docs/fix-live-restrictions/)); that is a vendor's account, but it means the home-builder web page should be linked from the profile, not shown as a code on stream. Creators must be 18 to go LIVE and viewers 18 to gift ([Streamer.Guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-rules)). Secondary reports say TikTok banned AI-generated voices in shopping livestreams in 2026 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-bans-ai-generated-voices-in-shopping-livestreams/822977/)); whether that affects a narrated game stream is unknown and needs checking against TikTok's current text.

On practical limits: TikTok publishes no fixed cash value for the diamonds creators receive, the working estimate is about half the coin value ([Streamer.Guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/tiktok-live-gifts-coins-diamonds)), and third parties agree on portrait 1080p at up to 60 frames per second with stream-key access gated and temporary ([Streamlabs](https://streamlabs.com/content-hub/post/how-to-go-live-on-tiktok); [Castr](https://docs.castr.com/en/articles/14107182-how-to-stream-to-tiktok-live-using-castr)). No figure for stream delay or maximum stream length was found.

## The strongest ideas for Zeus World, and where the owner's requests need care

### How the findings bear on what the owner asked for

**More to watch over many hours.** This is the best-supported request in the report. Give every citizen a trade, a daily routine and a quirk; let buildings and stalls attract citizens by need; add a deck of small story events; and put a pacing director over it with peaks and enforced quiet stretches. All of it is ordinary deterministic code with no running cost.

**City levels that unlock things.** Supported by the progress research and the Pokémon GO and Rival Peak cases. Use tiered milestones that pay out for everyone, bars that never start at zero, and targets that scale to how many people are actually present.

**Damage and repair after a lost battle.** A recoverable setback is good drama, and RimWorld's director deliberately eases off after a loss. But the constitution says permanent achievements are never erased, so damage should be visible, temporary and never touch a viewer's home or a completed monument permanently. Repair should be a collective goal that free actions can complete. If only gifts can close the repair bar, the design recreates battle pressure and edges toward the "sympathy pressure" pattern TikTok names. This is a judgement call resting on inference.

**A surprise celebration after a win.** Supported: rare, highlighted moments are what viewers remember. The Ahn'Qiraj and Fortnite evidence says to replay it, in the next stream's opening recap and as a lasting marker in the city, so those who were absent still get it.

**A walk-through of the battle MVP's home.** This one conflicts with the constitution as TikTok defines MVP. Battle points come from gifts and TikTok rewards the top three contributors, so "MVP" is in effect the top gifter. Touring their home is a spend ranking by another name, an in-world reward for gifting that sits near "transaction-like exchange", and a public spotlight on a gifter of the kind the gifting research warns about. TikTok already shows its own gifter rankings on the stream; Zeus World does not need to add to them. Options that keep the idea: name a "hero of the siege" by a measure that free actions can win, honour several roles at once, or let the host choose and say why. The showcase camera itself is a good idea; Cult of the Lamb's gallery shows people value a place where their identity can be inspected. It works equally well for a citizen chosen for tenure or a notable story.

**A palace with queens the owner chooses.** The streamer as visible game master is a strength (Helldivers), and Twitch's former chief executive advised to "give individuals a chance to shine" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/building-games-that-are-fun-to-watch-fun-to-play-in-the-twitch-era)). The risks are perceived favouritism and choosing by spend. State the criteria openly, do not choose by gifts or by lottery, and consider making a reign a recorded, permanent honour that passes on, which fits "prestige is earned" and "nothing is erased". No source studied this directly.

## Conclusion

The research changes the picture of what Zeus World is competing with. Its rivals are not GTA and not the gift games sold by the dozen for TikTok. Its nearest relatives are Rival Peak, Helldivers' game-mastered war and a RimWorld colony watched by a crowd, and all three say the same thing: the simulation supplies events, a human gives them meaning, and viewers stay for characters and a stake they recognise as theirs. Zeus World already has the hardest ingredients: persistent named homes, a constitution that avoids the mistakes others were punished for, and a streamer who can be the game master without hiring one. What it lacks is the showrunning layer, meaning recaps, a pacing director, a legible "what is happening now", and a narrator that is safe to leave talking. That layer is mostly ordinary code and costs a few dollars a stream.

Three things remain unknown and are worth settling before building further on real TikTok events. First, read TikTok's current LIVE and monetisation rules on the official pages, in particular on gifts in exchange for in-stream benefits, AI voices and on-screen links. Second, measure the real delay between a viewer's tap and what they see. Third, decide whether the project accepts resting on an unofficial data feed. A fourth question has no outside answer: nobody has published a study of a persistent audience-built world running for weeks. Zeus World will be producing that evidence itself, so it should record from the first real stream who returns, what they did, and which moments chat talked about.
