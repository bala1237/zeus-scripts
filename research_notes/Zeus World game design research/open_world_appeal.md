# Open-world appeal and what transfers to a watched, collectively steered world

Research date: 22 November 2026. Source dates are given inline. Where a finding rests only on a search-result summary of a page (the page itself was not opened), it is marked "(summary only)". Everything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning, not something a source says.

## 1. What do designers and analysts say makes GTA-style worlds compelling?

### Takeaway
Rockstar's own account is "build a fun place first, then hang content on it": a hand-made, condensed world packed with interesting locations, ambient life, optional random events and a very large audio layer. Most of the evidence is from 2013-2018 press interviews; Rockstar publishes almost no technical design talks, so the "why it works" is largely stated by its developers in general terms rather than measured.

### Cited Findings
- World first, missions second. Art director Aaron Garbut describes starting from a literal map rather than a design document: "We design a world that we think is fun and just try to include as many interesting locations as possible"; mission placement follows the map (summary only). — [MCV, Garbut interview](https://mcvuk.com/?p=80936)
- Condensation over scale. Dan Houser said the team "opted to condense the city's spread into an area players could comfortably traverse" to capture "the essence of what's really there in a city, but in a far smaller area". The GTA V map is 49 square miles (130 km2), about one eightieth of Los Angeles County. — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Not a 1:1 copy. Garbut said the team was not "dictated by reality"; Los Angeles was a starting point. Research trips produced about 250,000 photographs and many hours of video. — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Ambient life to avoid emptiness. Leslie Benzies said the countryside was populated with wildlife to avoid a "hollow" area. — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Random events. GTA V carried over "dynamic missions" from Red Dead Redemption: events that appear while exploring and can be accepted or ignored (example: an armoured van the player may intercept). — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Audio as a pillar. The team first planned to license over 900 tracks and refined that to 241, spread across fifteen music stations plus two talk stations; the original score runs over twenty hours; the 2014 re-release added more than 162 songs. — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Multiple protagonists existed partly to cover more activities: Benzies said the three-character structure was made "integral to the structure of the gameplay as well as the narrative"; search coverage also quotes him that "one character alone could not manage all the activities and tasks we wanted" (second quote summary only). — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Scale of effort. GTA V: core team of 360 at Rockstar North, over 1,000 across Rockstar studios, about three years of full production; analyst estimate of over US$137 million development budget and over US$265 million including marketing. — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018): about 500,000 lines of dialogue, 300,000 animations, 1,200 actors for motion capture of whom 700 also recorded dialogue, and a main-story script of about 2,000 pages, per Dan Houser's Vulture interview as reported. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/red-dead-redemption-2-has-a-60-hour-story-500000-lines-of-dialogue); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/76927-red-dead-redemption-2-details-revealed-60-hours.html)
- RDR2 design leads (Imran Sarwar, Rob Nelson) framed the goal as a world that feels "cohesive and organic", with every NPC able to be greeted or antagonised and with deeper interiors (summary only). — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-talks-about-rdr2-evolved-npcs/); [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/red-dead-redemption-2s-npc-interactions-are-a-huge-leap-forward-says-rockstar)

### Inferences
- Rockstar's formula, as its own people describe it, is about place before plot: a dense, condensed, hand-authored location, ambient life everywhere, optional interruptions, and a strong soundscape. None of those four depends on the viewer holding a controller.
- "Condense the essence into a small area" is directly applicable to a miniature city: the lesson is that perceived richness comes from interesting locations per unit of space, not from size.
- The GTA audio lesson (radio as the world's voice and tone) maps naturally onto an AI narrator plus in-world music and crowd sound. This is my mapping; no source makes it.
- The budget and headcount figures show that GTA's ambition is not reachable through content volume by a small team. The reachable part is the structure (place, life, events, voice), not the quantity.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative developer source on GTA's satire and tone as a design principle, and none quantifying GTA's pedestrian or ambient-activity systems. Claims about satire being central to GTA's appeal are common in criticism but I did not retrieve a citable one.
- The frequently repeated "each RDR2 NPC has an 80-page script" claim comes from a headline; Houser reportedly clarified his remarks to Kotaku and I could not retrieve the clarification, so treat it as unverified. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-worked-100-hours-weeks-rdr2/)
- RDR2 team size: a figure of 1,600 appears only via a Wikipedia talk page; not confirmed from a primary source. — [Wikipedia talk page](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Talk:Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- No academic game-studies paper on GTA's appeal was retrieved in this pass.

## 2. How do living-world simulations create emergent stories that are fun to watch?

### Takeaway
The strongest designer-authored theory is Tynan Sylvester's: the simulation does not need to be deep, it needs to project a readable model into the observer's head and let human pattern-seeking (apophenia) supply the meaning. Named characters, small casts, primal stakes and a pacing "storyteller" matter more than simulation fidelity, and that argument is about perception, so it holds for watchers as much as players.

### Cited Findings
- The Player Model Principle: "The whole value of a game is in the mental model of itself it projects into the player's mind." Anything in the simulation that "doesn't copy into the Player Model is worthless." (Tynan Sylvester, 2 June 2013.) — [Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)
- Deep simulation historically failed to be noticed: Ultima Online's virtual ecology was cut because players "just killed everything"; Sylvester adds that complex systems become hard to "understand, predict, or play with". — [Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)
- Apophenia: "seeing meaningful patterns in random or meaningless data." The simulation "does the logistics" and the human "adds the meaning and pathos"; "The game is a co-author, not an author." — [Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)
- "Hair complexity": "pieces of the simulation that don't affect anything outside themselves" (appearance descriptions in Dwarf Fortress, prisoner histories in Prison Architect). Cheap to build, adds flavour without system complexity. — [Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)
- Story-richness: "The percentage of interactions in a game that are interesting to the player." Real life is mostly not story-rich, so a simulation must be narratively condensed. "Choose the minimum representation that supports the kinds of stories you want to generate." Centre on human values such as "life/death, alone/together, wealth/poverty" and on primal rather than subtle emotions. — [Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)
- RimWorld was defined by its studio "not as a game, but as a story generator" (GDC 2017 talk "RimWorld: Contrarian, Ridiculous, and Impossible Game Design Methods"). — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024686/-RimWorld-Contrarian-Ridiculous-and)
- Sylvester's techniques for triggering apophenia include naming every character and keeping the cast small, around a dozen colonists (summary only). — [Game Developer, How RimWorld fleshes out the Dwarf Fortress formula](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-rimworld-i-fleshes-out-the-i-dwarf-fortress-i-formula)
- Pacing is delegated to selectable "narrators" (Cassandra Classic, Phoebe Chillax, Randy Random) that "each pace and choose the events in a different way". Simple visuals work because viewers fill in feeling, but this "can only work effectively if players have spent a long time with their characters and have bonded with them emotionally". Stories come from "spiralling events" that "strike a balance between player agency and unexpectedness". (Henri Brouard, 9 October 2019; a community blog, not a developer source.) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rimworld-dwarf-fortress-and-procedurally-generated-story-telling)
- Dwarf Fortress: stories arise when residents act outside their assigned duties and become actors in their own stories (Gamasutra Q&A with Tarn Adams, summary only). The community motto is "losing is fun". — [Gamasutra Q&A](https://gamasutra.com/design/q-a-dissecting-the-development-of-i-dwarf-fortress-i-with-creator-tarn-adams); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rimworld-dwarf-fortress-and-procedurally-generated-story-telling)
- Tarn Adams (GDC 2025 coverage) on why dwarves generate good stories: they are "allowed to embody bigger emotions", "allowed to make bigger mistakes". He has also discussed why mechanics-driven emergent stories are endearing where machine-written text has not yet produced a story people love (summary only). — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/dwarf-fortress-dwarves-are-more-human-than-human-creator-says-theyre-allowed-to-embody-bigger-emotions-theyre-allowed-to-make-bigger-mistakes-theyre-allowed-to-do-anything); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/game-development/dwarf-fortress-creator-says-we-have-a-natural-lean-towards-human-made-stories-but-but-were-all-going-to-be-very-sorely-tested-in-the-coming-years-by-ai/)
- The Sims: Will Wright's stated position is that "Players tell stories... We, designers, provide a platform for player expression", and that he wants to be "a story-enabler" rather than a storyteller (summary only; the first quote is from a lecture write-up, the second from a PC Gamer BAFTA report). — [Raph Koster, SXSW 07 Will Wright keynote notes](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=1059); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/will-wright-at-bafta-the-creator-of-the-sims-on-his-influences-and-hints-to-his-next-game)
- Small trait labels (for example "insomniac", "evil") are enough for people to extrapolate full characters in The Sims (community-written analysis, summary only). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-sims-part-1-how-playing-with-dolls-can-create-amazing-stories)
- Streamers already turn RimWorld into an audience-as-citizens show: the Twitch Toolkit mod family lets chat buy colonists, trigger events and gift gear using a coin economy and karma system that rewards participation; a separate mod, Puppeteer, lets viewers control a colonist live (summary only). — [Steam Workshop, Twitch Toolkit](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/1718525787); [Puppeteer listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fpardeike%2Fpuppeteer)
- An exploit fix in that mod's changelog (viewers bypassing a trait limit) shows audiences do probe viewer-facing economies for loopholes (summary only). — [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/1718525787?p=2)

### Inferences
- Sylvester's theory is the most transferable finding in this whole set, because it is about what an observer perceives. A viewer who only watches still builds a mental model and still performs apophenia. The design question becomes: what does the viewer's model contain, and how quickly can a newcomer build it?
- The cast-size finding cuts against a "thousands of named citizens" ambition. If attachment needs time with a small number of known characters, a large city needs a way to keep a small, rotating foreground cast (one's own citizen, neighbours, a few city characters) while the rest are crowd.
- A named citizen owned by a viewer is a ready-made attachment device: the viewer already supplies the bond that Brouard says normally takes hours.
- "Hair complexity" is the cheap win: backstories, trades, quirks and relationships that do not need to drive systems but give the narrator material.
- The RimWorld "storyteller" is the closest existing analogue to an AI narrator that also directs pacing. The evidence supports a director that decides when events happen, not only a voice describing them.
- Adams's caution suggests that stories people cherish come from mechanics that really happened in the world. A narrator that invents events the simulation did not produce risks feeling hollow. This is my reading of his remarks, not a tested result.
- "Losing is fun" depends on disaster and loss. A community-built persistent city where people own homes cannot casually destroy them, so the drama has to come from setbacks that are recoverable.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable viewership statistics for RimWorld, The Sims or Dwarf Fortress streams and let's-plays, and no study explaining their popularity as watched content. The popularity is widely asserted but unsourced here.
- I could not open Rock Paper Shotgun's "How RimWorld Generates Great Stories" (12 August 2016), which is likely the best journalistic source on the storyteller system. — [archived reader link](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2016/08/12/how-rimworld-generates-great-stories)
- Nothing was retrieved on Kenshi or Crusader Kings. Treat both as uncovered.
- Dwarf Fortress team size and sales were not verified in this pass (search coverage says only that Tarn and Zach Adams have built it for about two decades). — [PC Gamer roundtable](https://pcgamer.com/dwarf-fortress-and-caves-of-qud-roundtable-the-masters-of-simulation-talk-roguelikes-ai-and-making-the-infinite-compelling)
- The GDC 2017 RimWorld talk itself was not viewed; its content is known here only through write-ups.

## 3. What techniques make a world feel alive cheaply?

### Takeaway
The documented techniques are about arranging attention rather than adding content: something new within a fixed interval of travel, landmarks of graded prominence that pull the eye in several directions, things half-hidden so they are revealed, and scripted daily routines for inhabitants. The developers who used density rules also warn that filler placed only to hit the quota was a mistake.

### Cited Findings
- The Witcher 3 "40 second rule": CD Projekt Red's world team required that the player see something of interest roughly every 40 seconds. Bartosz von Ochman (Noclip documentary, 2017): "We did some tests and we found the player is focused on the stuff which we produce. So we have our rule of 40 seconds" (summary only). — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/59420/witcher-3s-40-second-rule-kept-players-engaged/index.html)
- The limit of that rule: quest designer Philipp Weber later said he regretted the smugglers' caches added in late 2014 to make the large maps feel less barren, concluding it was not in players' interest (summary only). — [GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/contents/6828-the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-dev-says-he-went-overboard-points-of-interest-on-the-map)
- A university thesis examines the rule: "The 40 Seconds Rule and Points of Interest in The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt" (Uppsala). Not opened; listed as a lead. — [Thesis record](https://openaccess.library.uitm.edu.my/Record/ndltd-UPSALLA1-oai-DiVA.org-uu-446386)
- One podcast summary gives the figure as 30 seconds; the 40-second figure is the one attributed to a named developer. — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/59420/witcher-3s-40-second-rule-kept-players-engaged/index.html); contradicted by [Podwise summary](https://podwise.ai/dashboard/episodes/7839749)
- Breath of the Wild "triangle rule" (Nintendo, CEDEC 2017, translated by Matt Walker): terrain is built from triangles in three sizes. The largest are landmarks, the medium ones block the view and hide what is behind, the smallest set the tempo of moment-to-moment play. A triangle offers a choice (over or around) and conceals something so the designer can surprise. Rectangles hide things completely. — [Translation of CEDEC 2017 talks](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399)
- Breath of the Wild "gravity": the first design spread towers evenly with events between them; playtesters felt guided, the game felt linear, and heat maps showed players following the same lines. The fix was to place structures of differing visibility and importance so they pull people in different directions and let them get sidetracked; the second heat map showed a much more even spread and led to what the team called "infinite play". No numbers are given for either map. — [Translation of CEDEC 2017 talks](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399)
- Chained reveals: a hill hides a bridge structure which hides a tower, giving a sequence of interest from one viewpoint. — [Translation of CEDEC 2017 talks](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399)
- Breath of the Wild audio: overworld music is not looped; music plays at special places and random phrases break the monotony; environmental sound drops out momentarily in battle. — [Translation of CEDEC 2017 talks](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399)
- Secondary coverage of the same talk. — [Kotaku, 2017](https://kotaku.com/breath-of-the-wilds-biggest-design-secret-lots-of-tria-1819113140); [Game Maker's Toolkit](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/how-nintendo-solved-zeldas-open-world)
- Scripted daily routines in RDR2: NPC days are scripted from waking to work to sleep; a fan channel followed three NPCs through a full in-game day (one leaves for work around 8 a.m., works a labouring job, drinks at the saloon, talks to others, sleeps) (summary only; press and fan documentation, not a Rockstar technical account). — [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.nl/red-dead-redemption-2-following-npcs-for-full-day-video-2018-11); [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/3-npcs-red-dead-redemption-2-einen-tag,286499)
- Bethesda's Radiant AI (Oblivion, 2006) gave NPCs daily schedules and generated conversations; Skyrim's NPCs "eat, sleep, work". Designer Bruce Nesmith reportedly said Skyrim's version was smoother and less noticeable, while Oblivion's quirkiness made NPC behaviour memorable (summary only; Nesmith via a secondhand report). — [Wikipedia, Radiant AI](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Radiant_AI); [UESP](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Oblivion:Radiant)
- GTA V and Red Dead use optional random events encountered while roaming (see section 1). — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)

### Inferences
- The 40-second rule translates to camera time rather than travel time for a watched world: within any stretch of roughly that length, the frame should contain something that changes or invites a question. The exact number is not validated for viewers.
- The gravity idea translates to the camera and to chat: several simultaneous points of differing prominence (a festival, a building going up, a small quarrel in a side street) give the audience something to argue about and steer toward, instead of one obvious focus.
- Concealment and reveal can be done with camera moves in a miniature city: a temple emerging from behind a hill, a street that opens on to the agora.
- Scripted schedules are cheap and effective. The RDR2 evidence is that viewers enjoy following one ordinary inhabitant through a day; a "follow this citizen" camera is supported by that behaviour.
- The Nesmith remark suggests that slightly odd, visible behaviour is more memorable than smooth realism. For a stream, visible quirk is probably worth more than fidelity.
- Weber's regret is a warning for a participation economy: content added only to fill space or give people something to spend on reads as filler.

### Gaps
- No source gave concrete density numbers for Breath of the Wild (distances, objects per area) or for Skyrim; the well-known rules are qualitative.
- I found no developer source on day/night cycles specifically as a liveliness technique.
- No primary Rockstar technical description of its schedule system exists in what I retrieved.
- The Radiant AI "packages" mechanism and what was cut between Oblivion's demo and release were not sourced.

## 4. Which qualities depend on first-person control, and which transfer to a spectated, collectively steered world?

### Takeaway
Research on audience participation says viewers are "neither fish nor fowl": they want visible impact and social connection, both of which are hard at scale because of stream delay and the dilution of any one voice. The evidence favours designing for social agency (collective goals, recognisable identity, clear evidence of each person's contribution) and for a performer or narrator who turns audience actions into story, over trying to give each viewer direct control.

### Cited Findings
- Definition and framing. Audience Participation Games have "a mutually-aware group of audience members who participate in a way that has a meaningful impact on the game". Participants "can influence the game's outcome through their participation, yet they have limited agency". (Seering et al., ACM DIS 2017, Carnegie Mellon.) — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Five participant motivations found in playtests (35 participants, sessions of 5 to 15): Helpers, Power-seekers, Collaborators, Solipsists and Trolls. Trolls turned to hindering "mainly out of boredom"; one said typing the same helpful commands was boring. — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Individual versus social agency. Individual control "encounters significant problems when there are thousands of audience participants vying for control"; designers "may consider granting participants social agency... where collaboration leads to collective power and collective rewards". — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Delay destroys the sense of impact. Stream delay meant responses were seen "between six and more than twenty seconds later"; with votes tallied every thirty seconds it was "almost impossible for viewers to know whether their vote would count". — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Voting did not build community by itself: participants spammed votes rather than forming blocs, and only 10 conversational messages passed between users across the sessions. Chat lacks identity markers "such as avatars, character level, and guild tag", so the scaffold for bonds "must come from game design rather than the platform". — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Richer choices engage more: conditions where the audience could choose to help or hinder produced "qualitatively richer gameplay dynamics"; audiences helped the streamer when behind and hindered when ahead, in effect balancing the drama themselves. — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- "Performable moments": audience effects gave the streamer situations to narrate and react to; designers should ensure "streamers can easily understand and summarize audience contributions". — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Visible attribution: "mechanics that show clear evidence of an individual's contribution will be more likely to capture that individual's interest." — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Low mechanical agency can still be enjoyed: the paper cites Progress Quest and Achievement Unlocked (over 4.6 million plays) as games with deliberately limited or meaningless options. — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- The paper explicitly excludes pure betting on an automated fight stream from "participation", because the game would proceed identically with no audience. — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- Follow-up work found streaming interfaces are not designed for audience participants and that latency between stream and chat interferes with feedback (Glickman et al., 2018; summary only). — [Glickman et al. 2018](https://josephseering.github.io/papers/Glickman_etal_2018_Design_Challenges.pdf)
- Twitch Plays Pokemon (2014): a peer-reviewed analysis found that a fraction of players who do not follow the crowd's average behaviour was key to progress, and frames the behaviour through social identity theory (Aleta and Moreno, EPJ Data Science 2019; summary only). — [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09730)
- A 2014 Trinity College Dublin dissertation argues Twitch Plays Pokemon produced coherent emergent narrative (invented theology and politics) through community interpretation of chaotic events (summary only). — [McGee 2014](https://publications.scss.tcd.ie/theses/diss/2014/TCD-SCSS-DISSERTATION-2014-036.pdf)
- Why people watch at all: a survey (N = 1,097) found five motivation types predict viewing hours, with social-integrative motives the main predictor of subscribing (Sjoblom and Hamari, Computers in Human Behavior; summary only). — [Sjoblom and Hamari](https://utupub.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/171134/Why%20do%20people%20watch%20others%20play%20video%20games.pdf?sequence=1)
- A larger survey (N = 2,227) identified six motivations: social interaction, sense of community, meeting new people, entertainment, information seeking, and lack of external support in real life; viewers of smaller channels were more socially motivated (Hilvert-Bruce et al., 2018; summary only). — [Tampere University summary](https://webpages.tuni.fi/gamification/2018/09/14/social-motivations-of-live-streaming-viewer-engagement-on-twitch/)
- Practitioner rules for watchability (Amazon's Breakaway, Mike Willette, 21 June 2017): first-time viewers stay for gameplay understanding, tension and suspense, and relatedness; show the whole arena; create "a single point of focus"; action within 5 to 10 seconds; rare, hard, outcome-changing moments are the memorable ones and should be highlighted with replays and slow motion. Twitch CEO Emmett Shear's advice: "you need to give individuals a chance to shine." — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/building-games-that-are-fun-to-watch-fun-to-play-in-the-twitch-era)
- Other practitioner lists: an obvious score, the possibility of comebacks, visual clarity, real-time action, close calls (Bombball post); fully deterministic games are less interesting to watch (slide deck) (summary only). — [Game Developer, Designing a New eSport](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-a-new-esport); [Slide deck](https://pt.slideshare.net/civax/not-only-esports-how-to-design-games-that-are-fun-to-watch)
- GDC 2019, Johanna Pirker, "Tackling Audience Experiences in Games": attract and teach spectators, retain with drama and unpredictability, involve through social interaction (summary only). — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025825/Tackling-Audience-Experiences-in)
- Always-on generated worlds: "Nothing, Forever" (an endless AI-generated sitcom on Twitch, launched 14 December 2022) averaged about four concurrent viewers before press coverage, then reached roughly 11,000 to 15,000 concurrent viewers in early February 2023; it was suspended on 6 February 2023 for at least 14 days after a character made hateful jokes; trackers later show single-digit to low-double-digit averages (snapshot dates unclear). — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/thousands-of-people-cant-stop-watching-ai-tv-show-nothing-forever/); [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/AI-twitch-series); [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/ai-generated-sitcom-nothing-forever-booted-from-twitch); [TwitchTracker](https://twitchtracker.com/watchmeforever)
- The same show was later reported stuck with characters walking into a fridge for at least five days before anyone fixed it (summary only). — [inkl / PC Gamer syndication](https://inkl.com/news/ai-driven-perpetual-seinfeld-falls-into-further-ruin-its-puppets-stuck-walking-into-a-fridge-for-at-least-five-days-before-anyone-fixed-it)
- Idle games: Kongregate's Anthony Pecorella (GDC 2015) reported retention on idle games as "amazing"; the genre traces to Progress Quest (2002) (summary only). — [Kongregate developer blog](https://blog.kongregate.com/idle-games-mechanics-and-monetization-of-self-playing-games); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022066/Idle-Games-The-Mechanics-and)

| Quality of open worlds | Depends on holding the controller? | Transfers to a watched, collectively steered world? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moment-to-moment feel (driving, shooting, traversal) | Yes, entirely | No. This is the largest part of GTA's appeal that cannot be carried over. |
| Personal freedom to go anywhere and do anything | Yes | Only weakly. Replace with collective steering plus a personal stake (own citizen, own home). |
| Private discovery and exploration | Mostly | Partly. Becomes shared discovery: camera reveals, new districts unveiled to everyone at once. |
| Mastery and skill expression | Yes | No, except as knowledge of the world and status within the community. |
| Mischief and transgression (causing chaos) | Yes | Risky. In a shared persistent world it becomes griefing of other people's property; needs bounded outlets. |
| Dense, condensed, hand-made place | No | Yes, strongly. |
| Ambient life and daily routines | No | Yes, strongly; arguably better watched than played, since viewers have time to notice. |
| Emergent stories from simple systems | No | Yes, strongly. Apophenia works for observers. |
| Random events and set pieces | No | Yes. They are the shared "did you see that" moments, and should be rare and highlighted. |
| World reactivity | Partly | Yes, if the reaction is visibly attributed to the people who caused it. |
| Audio, radio, tone, satire | No | Yes. The narrator and soundscape carry this. |
| Long-term persistence and ownership | No | Yes, and stronger than in single-player games because the stake is social. |

- The central design risk is the one Seering's study measured: a viewer acts and cannot tell that it mattered. With TikTok LIVE delay likely comparable to the 6 to 20+ seconds measured on Twitch, effects should be slow, persistent and labelled (a building that carries the contributor's name) rather than fast and fleeting. I did not find a measurement of TikTok LIVE delay.
- Persistent named citizens with homes supply exactly the identity markers the study says chat lacks. That is a structural advantage over the prototypes in the research.
- Boredom produced trolls in the study. Repetitive single-button participation is likely to do the same; a choice with a trade-off is better than a tap.
- Collective goals with collective rewards (the study's "social victories") are better supported by the evidence than per-viewer control.
- The practitioner rules on first-time viewers matter more on TikTok than on Twitch, because viewers arrive mid-stream constantly: someone landing cold should understand what the city is, what is happening now and what the stakes are within seconds. A persistent on-screen "what is being built / what just happened" is the equivalent of the obvious score.
- A single point of focus (Willette) and multiple pulls of attention (Nintendo's gravity) are in tension. A reasonable reading is one clear headline event at a time with secondary activity visible around it.
- "Nothing, Forever" shows that a generated always-on world can attract a five-figure audience on novelty, that novelty alone did not hold that audience, that unmoderated generated speech can get a channel suspended, and that an unattended simulation can sit broken for days. For an AI narrator on a platform with strict moderation, output filtering and a stuck-state watchdog are requirements, not polish.
- Idle-game retention suggests that slow visible progress which continues while the viewer is away is itself a draw; a city that has visibly grown since the last visit uses the same mechanism.

### Gaps
- I found no design literature specifically on zoo, ant-farm or "cozy webcam" style worlds, and nothing citable on auto-battler spectatorship. These remain uncovered.
- Twitch Plays Pokemon headline numbers (participants, peak viewers, duration, the anarchy and democracy voting modes) were not retrieved from the paper itself; the publisher's page would not load. The preprint is at the arXiv link above.
- Day[9]'s GDC Next 2013 talk "The Observer Friendly Game" and Pirker's GDC 2019 talk are behind GDC Vault and were not viewed.
- All the audience-participation research retrieved is from Twitch between 2014 and 2021. I found nothing specific to TikTok LIVE, whose audience behaviour, session lengths and gifting mechanics differ.
- No study was found of a persistent, multi-week, audience-built world; Seering et al. note their own sessions were single one-hour sittings with a transient community and call out long-term commitment as unstudied.
- The "Nothing, Forever" follower counts conflict between sources (over 156,000 versus about a thousand), so no follower figure is reported.

## 5. Concrete numbers and calibration for a small team

### Takeaway
The flagship open worlds are the product of a thousand or more people and budgets in the hundreds of millions, whereas the story-generator games that are most relevant to a watched world were built by one to a handful of people using abstraction, names and pacing instead of content volume. The numbers argue for borrowing GTA's structure and RimWorld's methods.

### Cited Findings
- GTA V (2013): core team 360, over 1,000 in total, about three years of full production; development estimated at over US$137 million, with marketing over US$265 million; map 49 square miles; about 250,000 reference photographs; 241 licensed tracks at launch across 15 music stations and 2 talk stations; over 20 hours of score. — [Wikipedia, Development of Grand Theft Auto V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Grand_Theft_Auto_V)
- Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018): about 500,000 lines of dialogue, 300,000 animations, 1,200 motion-capture actors (700 with dialogue), main script about 2,000 pages; parts of the team reportedly worked 100-hour weeks at several points in 2018. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/red-dead-redemption-2-has-a-60-hour-story-500000-lines-of-dialogue); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/76927-red-dead-redemption-2-details-revealed-60-hours.html)
- Breath of the Wild (2017) production facts from the CEDEC talks: world split into 120 dynamically loaded map sections; 2 UI designers; three full project "laps"; 617 freezes in the first two-day test play; a new main build every 5 minutes. — [Translation of CEDEC 2017 talks](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399)
- The Witcher 3: one interest point about every 40 seconds of travel. — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/59420/witcher-3s-40-second-rule-kept-players-engaged/index.html)
- RimWorld: three starting colonists, three pacing narrators, a practical cast of around a dozen; reported at over a million copies sold by 2019 (the cast figure is summary only; the sales figure is from a community blog). — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/rimworld-dwarf-fortress-and-procedurally-generated-story-telling); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-rimworld-i-fleshes-out-the-i-dwarf-fortress-i-formula)
- Sylvester's worked example of why full simulation does not scale: in a village of a hundred where each villager has a relationship with every other, the relationships are too many for anyone to perceive. — [Game Developer, The Simulation Dream](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-simulation-dream)
- Watchable-match pacing from Breakaway: rounds capped at 4 minutes, best of five, action within 5 to 10 seconds, whole match under 20 minutes; the design was cut from 24 versus 24 to 4 versus 4 for readability. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/building-games-that-are-fun-to-watch-fun-to-play-in-the-twitch-era)
- Audience participation measurements: stream delay 6 to 20+ seconds; 30-second vote windows felt unresponsive; typical channel sessions of 5 to 15 participants. — [Seering et al. 2017](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2017_Audience_Participation_Games.pdf)
- "Nothing, Forever": about 4 average viewers before coverage, about 11,000 to 15,000 at peak in February 2023, then a collapse to very low numbers. — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/thousands-of-people-cant-stop-watching-ai-tv-show-nothing-forever/); [TwitchTracker](https://twitchtracker.com/watchmeforever)

### Inferences
- "The next GTA" cannot mean GTA's content volume for a small team. On the evidence it can mean GTA's structure: a condensed, characterful place; visible ambient life; optional events; a distinctive voice.
- The Breakaway reduction from 48 players to 8 and RimWorld's small cast point the same way: readability falls as the number of simultaneous actors rises. A city with many citizens needs deliberate foregrounding.
- The four-minute round is a hint about attention rhythm for watched content: short arcs with a clear resolution, nested inside the long persistent arc of the city. The specific length is from a competitive game and is not validated for a simulation.
- A sensible order of investment for a small team, given all of the above: first legibility for a cold viewer, then visible attribution of contributions, then scripted routines and "hair complexity" for citizens, then a pacing director for events, and only then deeper systems.

### Gaps
- RimWorld and Dwarf Fortress team sizes and current sales were not verified from primary sources in this pass.
- No numbers were found for how many ambient activities or NPC routine types GTA V or RDR2 contain.
- No cost or headcount data was found for any stream-first or audience-built world.
