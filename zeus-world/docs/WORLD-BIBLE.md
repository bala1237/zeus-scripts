## 1. What Zeus World is

**In one sentence.** Zeus World is a small country under a big sky where everybody has a place, and you can look in on it any night to see what its people are up to.

**What it represents.** The stream is a room Zeus built for people. Zeus World is that room given land, weather and neighbours. Its promise is the teaser's line: *everyone has a place*. The fictional people show what that looks like; the real community is invited to take a place among them.

**Why a viewer should care.** Not because it is beautiful. Because of the people in it:

- You get to know them. The fisherman lies about his fish every time. You start to wait for it.
- Things happen whether you are there or not. If you miss a night, you missed something, and someone will tell you about it.
- It is going somewhere. Quarrels get settled, secrets come out, people change.
- You can matter in it. What the community does becomes part of the country's record, for good.

**What it is not.** It is not a reward screen for battles, and not a tour. A battle or a big moment is weather passing over a country that was already alive.

**The feeling to aim for.** The comfort of a favourite series, the surprise of a world that does things on its own, and the sense that Zeus and Zeya are watching it with you, as curious as you are.

## 2. Three origin myths to choose from

Three different answers to "where did this place come from, and why is it the way it is". They are meant to be substantially different in feeling. Each says what it gives the stories, what Zeya is in it, and where the community fits. They can also be blended.

### Myth A: The Storm That Stayed

**The legend.** Long ago the storm wandered the whole earth and never rested, because everywhere it went people ran from it. One night a small band of travellers with nowhere left to go did not run. They were too tired. They sat down in the rain in an empty valley and shared what bread they had with whoever sat next to them. The storm had never seen that. It stopped above them to watch, and for the first time it did not move on. Where its first bolt struck the cliff the river broke through: the Falls of Zeus. The storm took a name, Zeus, and made one law for the valley: *whoever arrives is given a place*. The land has no edge because the law has no limit. Whenever someone new is welcomed, there is a little more country than there was the day before.

**Its heart.** Belonging. The land grows by welcome.

**What it gives the stories.** Arrivals matter: every traveller on the road is a small event. Hospitality is the highest virtue and its failures are the drama. The first travellers' fire is still kept burning somewhere, and the old families argue about whose ancestors shared the bread and whose only ate it.

**Zeya in it.** The storm watched for so long that its watching became someone. Zeya is the part of the storm that pays attention: curious, amused, and fond of the people it has been looking at for an age.

**The community in it.** Exactly the travellers of the legend. Arriving is the founding act, repeated every night. It matches "the land without end" literally: the country is as large as its welcome.

**Risk.** It is the gentlest of the three. It will need mischief added, or it could turn sweet.

### Myth B: The Wager

**The legend.** The gods were arguing, as they do. One side said mortals are only good while they are ruled and frightened: take away the kings and the punishments and they will have each other's throats within a season. Zeus said they were wrong, and to prove it he staked the one thing a god cannot get back: his word. He set aside a single country and took out of it everything the others relied on. No king. No fate. No punishment from the sky. He bound himself to rule nothing there: he may send weather, he may throw a bolt to make a point, he may watch. He may not command. The wager is whether people left entirely to themselves will build something worth keeping. It has not been settled. The other gods are still watching, and some of them cheat.

**Its heart.** Freedom, and proof. Every kindness in the valley is evidence. So is every squabble over a goat.

**What it gives the stories.** Everything counts, including the small and the silly, so comedy and stakes come from the same place. The inhabitants half know they are watched and have opinions about it. The other gods interfering explains strange visitors, bad luck and temptations. And it gives battles a meaning inside the world: a rival streamer is another god's country, and a battle is a challenge in the wager. Zeus's own storms and lightning from the remote are his permitted nudges.

**Zeya in it.** She keeps the tally. Somebody has to record the evidence, and she was made for it: exact, a little nerdy about it, and long since unable to stay neutral. "I am supposed to be impartial. I am not. Do not tell the other gods."

**The community in it.** Witnesses who become evidence. People who arrive and settle are the strongest proof Zeus has: they were not put there, they chose it.

**Risk.** Being watched and judged could feel cold if it is overplayed. It should stay playful: the people of the valley mostly forget about the wager and get on with lunch.

### Myth C: The Unfinished Country

**The legend.** The first people who lived here were makers. They built the country the way a mason builds a wall, stone by named stone, and they meant to finish it. They did not. Nobody knows why they stopped; what is left of them is the Old Stones in the forest, and a country that was never completed. Walk far enough in any direction and the land is still being decided: paths that were not there last year, a hill with no name, mist where the map should be. The old saying is that a thing here becomes solid when it is named and stays solid while it is told. Zeus is the name the makers gave to the fire they worked by; the lightning is that fire, still trying to finish the work.

**Its heart.** Wonder, and memory. The world is a work in progress and everyone living in it is, knowingly or not, completing it.

**What it gives the stories.** Exploration with a purpose: there really is something new over the hill. Naming matters, so discoveries are events. The makers' unfinished plan is a deep well of mysteries (what were they building, why did they stop, what did they leave half done). The Cold Fire, if it is ever told, belongs naturally here.

**Zeya in it.** The country's memory. She is the telling that keeps things solid, which is why she never stops talking.

**The community in it.** Finishers. What the community names, builds and remembers becomes part of the country for good.

**Risk.** "What is forgotten fades" must never touch a real person or anything they earned: the constitution forbids punishing absence. If this myth is chosen, fading applies only to wild land and legend, never to people or their places.

### How they compare

| | A: The Storm That Stayed | B: The Wager | C: The Unfinished Country |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feeling | Warm, human | Playful, high stakes | Mysterious, wondrous |
| Best at | Arrivals, friendship, emotion | Comedy, rivalry, battles, Zeus's role | Exploration, discovery, long mysteries |
| Zeya is | The storm's attention | The keeper of the tally | The country's memory |
| The community are | The travellers | The witnesses and the proof | The finishers |
| Explains the land without edge | Yes: it grows by welcome | Partly | Yes: it is not finished |
| Explains battles | Weakly | Strongly | Weakly |

**My leaning, for the owner to overrule.** B as the frame, with A's law inside it. The Wager gives the widest range of the tone he asked for (comedy, rivalry, suspense, epic moments), gives Zeus something true to his role as host (he watches and nudges, he does not command), and gives battles a place in the story. The law "whoever arrives is given a place" can be what the people of the valley chose for themselves, which is exactly the kind of evidence Zeus is hoping for. C's makers and the Old Stones can sit underneath as the older, unexplained layer, kept for later mysteries. **Choice.**

## 3. The regions

The places below all exist in the open world today. For each: what it is like, how its people live, what it means, and what kind of story happens there.

### Riverbend: the village where the road begins

- **The place.** Villas round a paved square with a well, a columned hall, a shrine, market stalls and workshops, across the river from everything else.
- **Its people.** Stall-keepers, craftsmen, families who have known each other for four generations and hold grudges for three.
- **Its character.** Nothing happens in Riverbend without the square knowing by noon. People here are proud that the road *begins* with them; the city says it ends with them.
- **A custom.** The first cup from the well each morning is poured on the ground for whoever is still on the road.
- **Stories that happen here.** Gossip, feuds over nothing, courtship conducted in full view of everyone, market-day comedy.

### The mill and the Old Ford

- **The place.** A watermill on a shelf at the river's edge, its wheel in the stream; a timber bridge and a ford; a jetty with a boat.
- **Its character.** The crossing is where the village stops and the world starts. People say goodbye here, and wait here.
- **A custom.** Nobody crosses the ford without touching the post on the near bank. Nobody remembers why. The miller says he knows and will not say.
- **Stories that happen here.** Partings and returns, quiet conversations, the miller's advice.

### The Shepherds' Hill

- **The place.** Stone huts with smoking hearths, flocks of sheep and goats, dogs, eagles circling above.
- **Its people.** Shepherds who speak rarely and see a long way. They watch the weather for everyone and are never thanked.
- **A custom.** They read the eagles. Two circling to the left means rain; nobody down below believes it until they are wet.
- **Stories that happen here.** Solitude, weather, lost animals, the long view of whatever is going on in the valley.

### First Sight Hill

- **The place.** The rise on the road where the city and the sea are seen for the first time.
- **What it means.** Every traveller stops here, and nobody tells them to. It is the valley's threshold: you arrive as whoever you were, and you walk down as someone who has seen it.
- **A custom.** You leave a stone. The cairn is taller than a man now.
- **Stories that happen here.** Arrivals. Decisions. The place people go to think, or to be found.

### The farms and the Olive Grove

- **The place.** Stone farmsteads with barns, haystacks, pens, threshing floors and beehives; and sixty old olive trees round an oil press.
- **Its people.** Farmers who believe the city would starve in a week without them and say so.
- **What the grove means.** The trees are older than anyone's records. Every family that settles is said to be given the care of one tree; an untended tree is the one shame the farms recognise.
- **A custom.** The first pressing of the year is a festival: the first oil is carried to every lamp on the road before anyone cooks with it.
- **Stories that happen here.** Harvests, festivals, rivalries between farms, slow romance.

### Olympus: the city by the sea

- **The place.** A walled city on the plain with a harbour, an agora, a temple, an amphitheatre and a lighthouse, and a town grown up outside its west gate.
- **Its people.** Merchants, officials, performers, old money on the Marble Stair and new arrivals at the gate.
- **Its character.** Olympus believes it is the centre of the world. The country lets it believe that. Everything is faster, louder and more expensive here, and everyone from elsewhere pretends not to be impressed.
- **The gate town.** The west gate is where the country meets the city: traders who cannot afford a stall inside, travellers who have not yet decided to stay. It is the liveliest place in the valley.
- **Stories that happen here.** Ambition, status, deals, public spectacles, the comedy of country people in town and town people in the country.

### Thalassa: the harbour town

- **The place.** A paved quay, three jetties, fishing boats, a market hall and homes, with gulls over all of it.
- **Its people.** Fishing families and sailors. They work by the tide, not the sun, and think everyone inland is soft.
- **Its character.** Thalassa is where news and strangers arrive from outside the country. Its people tell stories for sport, and the truth is treated as a starting bid.
- **A custom.** No boat leaves without someone on the quay to see it go. A boat that sails unwatched is bad luck for a year.
- **Stories that happen here.** Tall tales, arrivals by sea, storms, waiting for a boat that is late.

### The High Sanctuary and the Falls of Zeus

- **The place.** A temple on the plateau above an 84-metre waterfall, with the whole valley below it.
- **What it means.** Whichever origin is chosen, this is where it happened: the first bolt, the wager sworn, the makers' fire. It is the one place where people lower their voices.
- **Its people.** A keeper or two, and whoever has climbed up with a question.
- **Stories that happen here.** Big questions, vows, confessions, the epic moments. Used rarely, so that it keeps its weight.

### The Old Stones

- **The place.** The ruins of an older temple in a forest glade.
- **What it means.** Somebody was here before, and the valley does not agree about who. It is the country's unanswered question, and it should stay partly unanswered for a long time.
- **A custom.** Children are told not to go there, which is why every adult in the valley has been.
- **Stories that happen here.** Mysteries, discoveries, things found that should not be there.

### The forest, the road and the High Country

- **The forest.** Oak, pine, laurel and plane along the river. Easy to get lost in; used by people who want to.
- **The road.** A mile of paving from Riverbend to the west gate, with shrines and travellers' camps beside it. Rumours travel along it at walking pace and arrive changed (see section 8).
- **The High Country.** Mountains over a thousand metres in the west, snow at the top, where the river rises. Almost nobody has been. It is where the map stops, and the direction every adventure eventually points.

## 4. The cast

Eight recurring characters to begin with. The test for each: **could a viewer recognise them from across the square, with the sound off?** And with the picture off, from one line? Each has a look, a way of speaking, a habit that repeats, something they want, and something they are hiding or do not know about themselves.

The owner's eight examples were treated as the brief. Each concept below starts from one of them and is rooted in a place and a job the world already has. All are adults. Names are placeholders. **Choice** on every one.

### Phokas, "the Admiral": fisherman of Thalassa

- **Starts from:** the fisherman who exaggerates everything.
- **Who he is.** Owner of one small boat, which he calls the fleet. Fifty or so, broad, sunburnt, loud.
- **Look.** A faded blue captain's cloak far too grand for his boat, worn in all weather. Walks as if the quay were rolling.
- **Voice.** Booming, delighted with itself, drops to a whisper for the "true" part of any story.
- **The habit.** Every story grows each time he tells it. The fish, the storm, the number of sea monsters. He never repeats a figure.
- **What he wants.** To be believed, once, completely.
- **What is underneath.** One of his stories is true, and it is the one he never tells: the night he did not go out and a friend's boat did not come back. The exaggeration is how he keeps people from asking.
- **Runs through the series.** Zeya keeps a running count of the fish's size. One day he tells the truth and nobody believes him.

### Melitta: keeper of the wine stall on Riverbend's square

- **Starts from:** the sharp-witted woman who sees through everyone's nonsense.
- **Who she is.** Forty or so. Her stall by the well is the real town hall. She has heard every excuse the valley has.
- **Look.** Sleeves rolled, a red cloth over one shoulder that she snaps at people like punctuation. Never stops working while she talks.
- **Voice.** Dry, quick, low. Finishes other people's lies for them.
- **The habit.** She names the true reason for whatever someone has just said, in five words, and goes back to wiping cups.
- **What she wants.** To see the sea. She has lived a mile and a half from First Sight Hill her whole life and has never walked up it, and has a different reason every year.
- **What is underneath.** Everyone brings their troubles to her; nobody has ever asked for hers.
- **Runs through the series.** Why she will not climb the hill. Who finally walks up it with her.

### Kyrillos, "the Fair": trader at the west gate

- **Starts from:** the merchant who believes everything is negotiable.
- **Who he is.** Thirty-five, elegant on a small budget, sells whatever he has from a folding table outside the gate because he cannot yet afford a stall inside.
- **Look.** Many rings, all brass. A set of scales he carries everywhere and consults like an oracle.
- **Voice.** Smooth, warm, endlessly reasonable. Every sentence is an opening offer.
- **The habit.** He bargains over things that have no price: the time of day, an apology, how long a silence should last. He has never told a lie, only quoted a price.
- **What he wants.** A stall inside the walls, in the agora, with his name over it.
- **What is underneath.** He cannot simply be given something. Kindness with no terms frightens him.
- **Runs through the series.** His campaign to get inside the walls. His courtly, hopeless negotiations with Melitta, the one person who always gets the better of him.

### Theron: the miller at the Old Ford

- **Starts from:** the wise elder with an unexpected sense of humour.
- **Who he is.** Seventy, slow moving, flour to the elbows. People cross the river to ask his advice.
- **Look.** White with flour, always. A walking stick he does not need and uses to point at things.
- **Voice.** Slow, grave, gravelly: the voice of a man about to say something profound.
- **The habit.** He delivers jokes in exactly the voice he delivers wisdom, with no change of face, and lets people work out which it was. Half his famous sayings are things he made up to see if anyone would carve them on a wall. Some have been.
- **What he wants.** For someone to take over the mill who loves it, and not to have to ask.
- **What is underneath.** He knows more about the Old Stones than anyone alive and has decided, for a reason of his own, not to say.
- **Runs through the series.** The longest-running practical joke in the valley. What he knows. Who inherits the wheel.

### Kallia: mapmaker from the Shepherds' Hill

- **Starts from:** the curious young adventurer who constantly gets into trouble. **An adult, in her early twenties.**
- **Who she is.** A shepherd's daughter who refuses to be a shepherd. She is making the first true map of the country, and the country keeps disagreeing with it.
- **Look.** A map case on her back as long as her arm, ink on her hands, one sandal always newer than the other. Followed everywhere by a goat she claims is not hers.
- **Voice.** Fast, bright, starts sentences before she has finished the last one.
- **The habit.** "It will only take a moment." It never does. She returns from every trip with a discovery and a problem, usually the same thing.
- **What she wants.** To find where the river begins, up in the High Country, where nobody has been.
- **What is underneath.** She is afraid that if she stops moving she will end up on the hill with the sheep after all.
- **Runs through the series.** The map filling in. The goat. The expedition to the source, which is the series' first real adventure.

### Xanthe of the Marble Stair: the richest woman in Olympus

- **Starts from:** the wealthy individual who is surprisingly clueless about ordinary life.
- **Who she is.** Forty-five, heir to the grandest villa in the city, generous, cheerful and entirely unprepared for a world she has never had to touch.
- **Look.** White and gold, immaculate, and always holding some ordinary object as though it might bite: a loaf, a broom, a fish.
- **Voice.** Musical, enthusiastic, fascinated by everything. Pronounces common words as if for the first time.
- **The habit.** Each week she decides to learn "an honest trade" and arrives somewhere to be useful. She is a catastrophe, pays for all the damage, and has a wonderful time.
- **What she wants.** To be good at one thing with her own hands, and to be liked for something other than her house.
- **What is underneath.** She is lonely, and she is cleverer than she lets on: now and then she says something so exact that the whole square goes quiet.
- **Runs through the series.** Trade of the week. Her unlikely friendship with Melitta. The day she is actually good at something.

### Lampros: lamplighter of the city and the road

- **Starts from:** the village gossip who somehow knows everything.
- **Who he is.** Thirty, thin, always slightly out of breath. He lights every lamp from the agora to the last shrine at dusk and puts them out at dawn, so he is outside every window in the valley twice a day.
- **Look.** A long pole with a hooked flame, a ladder on his shoulder, soot on his nose. Seen in the distance at dusk as a moving point of light.
- **Voice.** A confidential murmur, quick, with a little gasp before the best part.
- **The habit.** "I am not one to talk, but." He trades one piece of news for another and keeps strict accounts. He is almost always right about what happened and always wrong about why.
- **What he wants.** To be the one something happens *to*, for once.
- **What is underneath.** He is the only person who sees the whole valley every single day, and he is kinder with what he knows than anyone gives him credit for. He has kept one real secret for years.
- **Runs through the series.** He is how news moves. He is also the natural first witness to anything strange that happens after dark (see section 7).

### Ione: the traveller

- **Starts from:** the mysterious traveller whose background is gradually revealed.
- **Who she is.** A woman of no clear age who came down the road one evening with a covered hand-cart and has not said where from. She camps at the travellers' fires and has not asked for a place.
- **Look.** A grey travelling cloak, a wide hat, the cart always covered. She stands very still when others fidget.
- **Voice.** Quiet, exact, a trace of an accent nobody can name. Answers a question with a better question.
- **The habit.** She pays for things with small favours and knows how to mend anything. She knows the old names of places that the valley has forgotten.
- **What she wants.** Something at the Old Stones. She is waiting for the right moment to go.
- **What is underneath.** For the owner to choose later, and worth keeping open for a long time. Candidates: she is the last of the people who built the Old Stones; she was born here and nobody recognises her; she was sent by one of the other gods of the Wager; she is looking for someone who came this way before her.
- **Runs through the series.** What is under the cover of the cart. Why the law of the valley (everyone is given a place) is the one thing she will not accept.

### How they connect

| | The relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Phokas and Melitta | Courted thirty years ago; it ended the year his friend's boat was lost. They bicker like people who are not finished |
| Kyrillos and Melitta | He negotiates; she wins. He is devoted to her in a way he would describe as "open to terms" |
| Xanthe and Melitta | The unlikeliest friendship in the valley. Melitta is the first person who ever told Xanthe she was wrong |
| Theron and Kallia | He is her grandfather's oldest friend. He wants her to take the mill; she wants him to tell her what he knows about the Stones. Neither will ask |
| Kallia and Lampros | Allies in trouble. She supplies the plan, he supplies the information, both are wrong |
| Lampros and Phokas | Rivals as the valley's storyteller. One exaggerates, the other reports; each thinks the other is unreliable |
| Kyrillos and Xanthe | He has spent a year trying to sell her something. She keeps trying to simply give him the money, which he cannot accept |
| Ione and Theron | He looked at her once, at the ford, a moment too long. They have not spoken since. Both avoid it |
| Ione and Kallia | Kallia has decided Ione is the most interesting thing on any map and will not leave her alone |

**Room for romance** (between fictional characters only): the old, unfinished thing between Phokas and Melitta; Kyrillos's devotion; and one new pairing to grow slowly on screen, to be chosen once the cast is settled.

**On the bench, for later:** the keeper of the High Sanctuary, who took a vow of silence and is very bad at it (the natural home of the philosophical conversations); the harbour-master of Thalassa, Phokas's nemesis, who writes everything down; twin potters who have not spoken to each other in ten years and share a workshop; a shepherd who has not come down the hill in so long that he is treated as weather.

## 5. Zeya in the world, and her storytelling guide

### She is the same Zeya

One character across everything Zeus does. Zeus World is something she knows about and does, not a new version of her. Her established manner, from her approved voice profile and promos: mostly natural talking; playful and teasing; a nerdy streak she enjoys ("I DO love a good calculation"); mischievous; and under it, sincere warmth about people ("A stranger becoming a familiar name"). She is the co-host who keeps things interesting.

Her older written character (in the co-host app, March 2026) is sharper and more glamorous: "high-glam, highly observant, sharp-tongued", teasing that "roasts situations, not people", with seven moods from Sassy Socialite to Soft Loyalty. The two descriptions differ in places, and which is the true Zeya is the owner's call (`knowledge/zeya.md`). The samples below are written between the two: sharp, playful, warm underneath. Two of her existing "lore pillars", The Storm and The Ledger, happen to fit Myths A and B.

Her existing rules carry over unchanged: she never says the word "gift" or "gifts"; she never states an amount or a ranking by spend; she does not talk over Zeus.

### Where she stands

She is not one of the inhabitants. She is beside Zeus and the audience, looking in, with a view of the whole country at once. Whichever myth is chosen gives the reason (the storm's attention, the keeper of the tally, the country's memory).

**Can the inhabitants hear her? Choice.** Three ways to play it:

1. **No.** She watches and comments; they never know. Simplest.
2. **Some can.** A few of them hear her now and then and argue back: Theron answers her without looking up; Kallia thinks it is the wind; Phokas claims she is a sea spirit who is in love with him. This is the funniest and lets her "occasionally participate", as the owner asked.
3. **All can,** as the voice of the sky. Powerful, but it makes her a ruler, which fits none of the myths.

My leaning is the second.

### Her five jobs

| Job | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| **Guide** | Bringing a newcomer in: what this place is, who that is, why they should care, in two lines, not ten |
| **Spotter** | "Hold on. Something is happening at the mill." She notices, and takes the picture there |
| **Commentator** | Reacting like a friend watching the same show: delight, disbelief, taking sides |
| **Zeus's partner** | Talking it over with him: teasing, betting on outcomes, disagreeing about who is in the right |
| **Participant** | Now and then, a word to a character, or a small act: she is not above nudging a lamp |

### How she tells it

- **She talks like a friend who has been watching all day, not a documentary.** "Okay. You need to see this." Never "Here we observe".
- **Short.** One thought, then let the scene play. Silence is part of her job.
- **She has favourites and admits it.** She is fond of Theron, exasperated by Phokas, and worried about Ione.
- **She knows more than she tells.** "I know what is in the cart. I am not telling you. Watch."
- **She keeps score, because she is a nerd.** The fish's size, Kyrillos's losses to Melitta, days since Xanthe last broke something.
- **She remembers.** "Last week he swore he would never set foot in Thalassa again. It is Tuesday."
- **She changes with the moment.** Light in the square, quiet at the ford, hushed at the Sanctuary. For the rare great moment she stops joking altogether, and that is how the audience knows it matters.
- **She does not explain the joke, and she does not narrate what the audience can see.**

### The two books

| | The Myth | The Chronicle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Fiction: legends, the cast, their stories | Fact: what real community members really did |
| Who writes it | Us, approved by the owner | Nobody. It is only recorded |
| How Zeya marks it | "They say..." "The story goes..." "In the valley they will tell you..." | Names, dates, plain statement: "On Friday night, forty of you saw this." |
| May she invent? | Within what is approved | Never |

A real person is never given words they did not say, a relationship, a quarrel or a part in a sensitive storyline. Real people choose how they appear, and can choose not to.

### Sample lines

- *Opening a stream, for newcomers:* "New here? Quick version. That is a country. Those are its people. None of them know you are watching, and at least two of them are about to do something foolish. Stay."
- *Introducing a place:* "First Sight Hill. Everybody stops here. Nobody tells them to. I have watched a great many people stop here and I still like it every time."
- *Introducing a character:* "The gentleman in the very large cloak is Phokas. He owns one boat. He will tell you it is a fleet. Do not argue, it only encourages him."
- *Spotting:* "Zeus. Zeus. The lamplighter just ran past the bakery. Lampros does not run. Can we go and look?"
- *The nerd:* "For the record, that fish has now grown four times since Monday. At this rate it reaches the size of the harbour by the weekend. I have a chart."
- *Sincere, rare:* "She has lived her whole life a short walk from that hill and never climbed it. I do not think it is the hill she is afraid of."
- *The Chronicle:* "That is not a story, by the way. That happened. Tuesday, during the storm, and I have the names of everyone who was here."

## 6. Sample scenes

Written the way they would be staged at first: two or three people, seen from a few paces, voices and captions, body language doing the work. Zeya's lines are marked. These are samples of tone, not approved scripts.

### Everyday 1: Morning at the well (Riverbend)

*Dawn. Melitta sets out cups. Lampros arrives with his ladder, putting out the square's last lamp.*

**Lampros:** I am not one to talk, but.
**Melitta:** You are exactly one to talk. Go on.
**Lampros:** The miller was at the ford before sunrise. Standing. Just standing.
**Melitta:** He is seventy. Standing is most of what he does.
**Lampros:** Facing the forest.
*She stops wiping the cup. One beat. Starts again.*
**Melitta:** What do you want for it?
**Lampros:** *(delighted)* What is the baker's daughter doing in Thalassa?
**Melitta:** Buying fish. Like a person. You are slipping, Lampros.
**Zeya:** She did not answer the question about the miller. She never does. I am writing that down.

### Everyday 2: The price of shade (the west gate)

*Noon. Kyrillos's folding table. A shepherd has stopped under his awning, out of the sun.*

**Kyrillos:** Friend. You are standing in my shade.
**Shepherd:** It is the gate's shade.
**Kyrillos:** The gate provides the wall. I provide the awning. What you are enjoying is a partnership. I ask only a fair share.
**Shepherd:** I have a cheese.
**Kyrillos:** *(consults his scales, with nothing on them)* For half the cheese, shade until the hour. For the whole cheese, shade and conversation.
**Shepherd:** What if I do not want the conversation?
**Kyrillos:** Then it is the whole cheese.
**Zeya:** He has never told a lie in his life. He has only ever quoted a price.

### Comic 1: The fish

*Thalassa quay. Phokas, arms wide. A small crowd. Lampros among them, counting on his fingers.*

**Phokas:** As long as this jetty. I say it plainly. I do not embroider.
**Lampros:** On Monday it was as long as your boat.
**Phokas:** It has had time to grow.
**Lampros:** It is dead, Phokas. You ate it.
**Phokas:** *(dropping to the whisper)* That is what makes it remarkable.
**Zeya:** Fifth version. I would like it noted that in the first one, there was no fish. He caught a boot.

*Community hook: chat settles, by vote, the official size of the fish for the week. The result goes on a board on the quay. Phokas contests it every time.*

### Comic 2: Xanthe learns to bake

*The bakery, Riverbend. Xanthe, immaculate, holding a sack of flour as if it were an infant. Melitta leaning in the doorway.*

**Xanthe:** And this is the flour! I have always wanted to meet it.
**Melitta:** It goes in the bowl.
**Xanthe:** All of it?
**Melitta:** No.
*A soft, enormous sound. A white cloud rolls out of the door. Silence.*
**Xanthe:** *(from inside the cloud, thrilled)* Is it meant to do that?
**Melitta:** No.
**Xanthe:** I shall pay for the bakery.
**Melitta:** You shall pay for the *street*.
*Theron walks past, white with flour as always, glances at Xanthe, white with flour.*
**Theron:** *(gravely)* Welcome to the trade.
**Zeya:** Days since Xanthe last broke something: zero. Again. I have stopped resetting the sign, I just leave it.

### Emotional: The hill

*Dusk. The foot of First Sight Hill. Melitta has closed the stall early. Xanthe finds her standing at the bottom of the path.*

**Xanthe:** You are going up?
**Melitta:** I am looking at it.
**Xanthe:** I am told the view is very fine. One can see the sea.
**Melitta:** So they say.
**Xanthe:** You have never been? It is just there.
**Melitta:** *(a long pause)* My mother used to say she would take me when the work was done. The work was never done. Then it was my work. *(dry again, not quite managing it)* It is a hill. It will be there.
*Xanthe says nothing clever. She sits down on the wall at the bottom of the path, in her white and gold, in the dust.*
**Xanthe:** Then I shall look at it with you. I am extremely good at not doing things.
*Melitta laughs once, surprised, and sits. The light goes. Up the road, a small flame: Lampros beginning his rounds.*
**Zeya:** *(quietly)* I am not going to say anything about that one.

### Dramatic: The cart

*Night, rain, a travellers' fire beside the road. Ione mending a strap. Kallia arrives soaked, map case over her head, the goat behind her.*

**Kallia:** I found something at the Stones. A mark, cut fresh. Not old. *(she unrolls a rubbing)* It is the same as the one on your cart.
*Ione does not look at the paper.*
**Ione:** You went under the cover.
**Kallia:** The goat went under the cover. I went after the goat.
**Ione:** What did you see?
**Kallia:** Nothing. It was dark. *(beat)* Something that looked back.
*Ione stands. She is taller than Kallia thought.*
**Ione:** Listen to me, mapmaker. You draw what is there. That is a good thing to be. But there are places in this country that were left off the map by people who loved it. Ask the miller why.
**Kallia:** Theron? What does Theron know?
*Lightning, far off over the Sanctuary. In the flash, the cover of the cart has moved.*
**Ione:** Go home, Kallia.
**Zeya:** ...Zeus? That was not you, was it? The lightning.

*The last line hands the moment to Zeus, live. Whatever he answers is the cliffhanger.*

## 7. A mystery across five episodes: The Lamp Nobody Lit

A small mystery that uses things the world already has (lamps at night, the road, the Old Stones), gives every cast member something to do, and ends with an answer and a new question. One episode a stream, a few minutes each.

**Episode 1: Somebody is doing my job.** At dawn Lampros finds a lamp burning at the first shrine on the road. He did not light it. Nobody believes him: he is the lamplighter, of course a lamp is lit. He is outraged to finally have something happen to him and have it be this. *Ends:* the next dawn there are two.

**Episode 2: The watch.** Kallia and Lampros stake out the road with a plan, a blanket and the goat. They argue about who it is, fall asleep, and wake to a third lamp lit and the goat gone. The goat is found at the third shrine, calm, with a strip of grey cloth tied round its neck. *Ends:* Kallia notices the lamps are getting closer to the forest.

**Episode 3: Everyone has a theory.** The square takes sides. Phokas saw the culprit: twelve feet tall, walked on the river. Kyrillos is selling "oil from the phantom's own lamp" at the gate. Xanthe offers to pay for whoever it is to have proper lamps. Melitta says nothing and watches Theron, who has looked at one of the lamps and gone very quiet. It is an old pattern, not made in anyone's lifetime. *Ends:* Lampros, alone at dusk, sees Ione on the road, walking toward the forest.

**Episode 4: The wrong answer.** Kallia confronts Ione at her fire. Ione did not light them. She has been following them too, and she is afraid of where they lead. That night the fourth lamp is lit, and this time Lampros was watching the road from both ends. Nobody passed. *Ends:* the fifth lamp will be at the Old Stones.

**Episode 5: The right answer, and a worse question.** They wait at the Stones, all of them, the whole cast for the first time in one place. Near midnight someone comes slowly up the path with a small flame, leaning on a stick he does not need. It is Theron. His wife kept the old lamps on the forest road until she died; every year, for the five nights before the day he lost her, he lights the road to the Stones, where they were married. He has done it for nineteen years. Nobody noticed, because nineteen years ago there was no Lampros to count lamps. Melitta knew. She has always known.

He is not sorry. "You watched my lamp with a goat," he says, gravely. Then he goes to light the fifth lamp, and stops.

It is already burning.

*The answer was human and kind. The question that remains is the series' next door: who lit the last one, and how long has Ione known?*

**Why it works for a stream:** each episode stands alone as a few funny minutes; a viewer who comes back learns something; a viewer who missed one asks what happened; and chat has something to argue about between streams.

**Community part:** guesses in chat are counted and shown as "what the valley thinks" (by character, never by person's spend). Everyone present on the fifth night is entered in the Chronicle as a witness.

## 8. Things the world does by itself

Examples of what should happen with nobody steering, once the world has a clock and its people have routines. Each has a cause in the world, not in TikTok.

| What happens | What causes it | Who it involves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The boats stay in | The weather turns | Thalassa fills its wine shop; Phokas has an audience; the stories get worse |
| Market day | The calendar | Riverbend's square is full; Kyrillos walks up from the gate to compete; Melitta is at her sharpest |
| The goat gets into the agora | Kallia is in the city, so the goat is | A chase through the stalls; the camera's best friend |
| A boat is late | Tide, wind, chance | The quay fills with people watching the horizon; the custom says someone must wait |
| A stranger comes down the road | The road always has travellers | Lampros sees them first; by noon Riverbend knows who they are, wrongly |
| The mill wheel jams | Wear | Theron will not ask for help; half the village arrives to not be asked |
| First pressing | The season | A festival at the Olive Grove; oil carried to every lamp on the road |
| A quarrel grows | Two people who fell out last week meet again | Whoever is nearby takes sides |
| The shepherds come down | The eagles circled left | Nobody listens; it rains; the shepherds say nothing, loudly |
| Lamps, dusk and dawn | The clock | Lampros's rounds: a moving light across the whole country, and a reason to be anywhere |

### The rumour road

A small machine for making comedy out of the world's own events. Anything that happens becomes a piece of news. News travels at walking pace, carried by whoever is on the road, and **each person who repeats it changes one thing**, according to who they are: Phokas makes it bigger, Lampros gets the reason wrong, Kyrillos puts a price on it, Xanthe misunderstands a word, Melitta cuts it back to the truth.

So a goat falling off the jetty at Thalassa at dawn reaches Riverbend by evening as a sea-bull that carried off a child of the harbour-master, and Zeya, who saw the goat, gets to follow the story up the road and watch it grow. The audience knows the truth and the characters do not, which is the oldest comic situation there is.

### What makes it worth watching, not only busy

The owner's warning was against activity for its own sake. The rule proposed: **nothing is shown unless somebody wants something.** A man carrying a sack is furniture. A man carrying a sack he was told not to touch is a scene. The world can be full of the first kind in the background; Zeya and the camera only stop for the second.

## 9. How the community takes part

Ways for real people to become part of the country without being turned into fiction, and without anything depending only on spending.

| What a person does | What the world does | Which kind of recognition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrives for the first time | A traveller appears on the road; Zeya says the name; a stone is added to the cairn on First Sight Hill | Presence |
| Keeps coming back | Given a place: a lamp on the road that is theirs, lit when they are in the room | Loyalty |
| Welcomes newcomers in chat, answers questions | Melitta sets out a cup "for the one who keeps the door". Named in the Chronicle as a host of the valley | Kindness |
| Joins a vote or a guess | "What the valley thinks" is counted and shown; the outcome changes the scene (the official size of the fish, which path Kallia takes) | Participation |
| Is there when something big happens | Entered in the Chronicle as a witness, with the date, for good | Presence |
| Names something Kallia discovers | The winning name goes on the map and stays; the Chronicle records who proposed it | Contribution |
| Wins one of Zeus's games | Their name on the games board; the cast hear of it as news | Skill |
| Supports in a battle | The top three honoured as already agreed (hero films, the triumph), without amounts | Support |
| Makes the room laugh | Zeya quotes the line back, by name, as the valley's saying of the night | Wit |

**Rules for this.**

- A real person appears in the Chronicle only: what they did, said in plain words. The cast may *hear of* a real person as news ("they say forty travellers came down the road tonight") but a real person is never given lines, feelings, a romance or a feud.
- Being represented is a choice. People can ask to be left out, or to be shown under another name.
- Names are both shown and spoken when it suits the moment. Recurring members can set how their name is said; until they have, Zeya asks rather than guesses when a name is hard.
- No recognition here can be had only by spending.

## 10. Fifteen minutes of Zeus World during a LIVE

An example of how a segment could run once phases 1 and 2 exist. Evening on the stream; dusk in the valley.

| Time | On screen | Who is talking |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 1:00 | Down to the west gate. Lampros lighting the gate lamp; Kyrillos packing up his table | **Scene (2 min):** Lampros trades a piece of news. Kyrillos tries to buy it wholesale. We learn that Phokas has told the fish story in Riverbend, and it has grown |
| 3:00 | Zeus and Zeya over a wide shot of the city | **Zeus and Zeya, live:** he asks how big the fish is now. She has a chart. Zeus opens the vote: chat sets the official size for the week |
| 5:00 | A fast low flight down the coast to Thalassa. Boats rocking, gulls, the quay at dusk | **Zeya** as guide: Thalassa in two lines, and its custom about watching boats leave |
| 5:45 | The quay. Phokas and the small crowd | **Scene (2 min 30):** "The fish". The vote's result is carried in and read out. Phokas contests it |
| 8:15 | Zeus is called away to greet people, read chat, play a track. The world carries on | **The world alone (3 min).** The camera drifts: lamps coming on across the country; the mill wheel; shepherds' fires. Zeya drops in twice, briefly: a returning regular's lamp lighting on the road (their name, spoken); "forty travellers on the road tonight" |
| 11:15 | Zeya interrupts herself | **Zeya, spotting:** "Hold on. Something is happening at the foot of the hill." The camera goes |

About seven and a half minutes of it is written scenes; the rest is the world and the two hosts.

## 11. What I would like the owner to react to

Nothing here needs a formal answer. Reactions are enough: "more of this", "not this", "I laughed", "too soft".

1. **The myth.** A, B, C, a blend, or none of them. Which made you want to hear more?
2. **The cast.** Who do you like at once, who is flat, who is missing? Is there a kind of person from your own stream's humour that belongs in the valley?
3. **The names.** Greek-flavoured placeholders. Keep the style, or something easier to say on stream?
4. **Zeya and the inhabitants.** Can none of them hear her, some, or all?
5. **The humour.** Are the comic samples in your register, or should it be broader, sharper, drier?
6. **How far the drama may go.** The samples stop at loss, secrets and fear. Are danger, villains and real conflict wanted in this country?
7. **The mystery.** Is "The Lamp Nobody Lit" the right size for a first one?
8. **The fifteen minutes.** Is that the balance you picture between written scenes, the world on its own, and you and Zeya talking?
