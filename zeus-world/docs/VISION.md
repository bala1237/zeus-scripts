# Zeus World: the long-term vision

Backlog: ZW-057. Given by the owner on 27 Nov 2026, in the brainstorm on voice-overs and story. This is the direction every later plan is measured against. Part 1 is his brief, word for word. Parts 2 to 5 are the assessment and proposals made in reply; **they are not decided until he says so, and nothing here is built.**

In one line (the owner): "Zeus World should eventually feel like an ongoing television series inside a living, open-world simulation, experienced live alongside Zeus and ZEYA."

## Part 1. The owner's brief, 27 Nov 2026

### Product direction

I want Zeus World to evolve into a living, breathing, open-world entertainment experience, taking inspiration from GTA's immersive world design, character interactions, emergent events and storytelling.

It should not simply be a beautiful 3D environment with cinematic camera movements and occasional narration. The environment is the foundation, but the inhabitants, their personalities, relationships, conversations and activities are what bring it to life.

The objective is to keep viewers curious about what is happening, what characters are talking about, what might happen next and how the world is evolving.

### 1. ZEYA as the narrator

We already have ZEYA, and she should be the primary narrator of Zeus World. We do not need to introduce another dedicated narrator at this stage.

ZEYA should have a deeper understanding of the world, including its geography, mythology, inhabitants, history, culture and ongoing events.

Her responsibilities should eventually include:

- Introducing Zeus World to new viewers.
- Explaining its purpose, history and mythology.
- Introducing locations and their significance.
- Introducing characters and their personalities.
- Commenting on interesting events happening within the world.
- Narrating ongoing stories and adventures.
- Conversing with Zeus about what is happening.
- Interacting with inhabitants of the world.
- Engaging the audience through humour, curiosity and storytelling.
- Remembering previous events and continuing stories across LIVE sessions.

Her narration should retain her established personality. She should not sound like a conventional documentary narrator.

### 2. A living world inspired by GTA

The world should gradually evolve towards a persistent simulation where inhabitants have their own identities, behaviours, motivations and relationships. For example:

- Merchants open their shops in the morning and close them at night.
- Fishermen prepare their boats and return with their catch.
- Villagers gather and discuss local events.
- Travellers arrive from distant regions.
- Characters develop friendships, disagreements and rivalries.
- Festivals and celebrations occur.
- Unexpected visitors arrive.
- Mysteries emerge naturally.
- Weather and environmental changes influence daily activities.
- Characters remember significant encounters and previous conversations.

The world should remain interesting even when Zeus is not actively controlling it and when there are no TikTok battles, gifts or other external events.

### 3. Character interactions and conversations

This is a major part of the entertainment experience.

I want viewers to observe interesting, humorous, emotional and occasionally dramatic conversations between inhabitants.

Characters should eventually have:

- Unique personalities and voices.
- Occupations and responsibilities.
- Personal histories.
- Relationships with other characters.
- Individual motivations and goals.
- Memories of previous interactions.
- Distinct conversational styles.
- Ongoing personal storylines.

ZEYA should be able to discover these interactions, direct the camera towards them, provide context and occasionally participate in conversations.

The conversations should be entertaining enough that viewers want to stay and hear what happens next.

We should avoid meaningless AI-generated dialogue simply to make characters appear active. Conversations need purpose, personality and entertainment value.

### 4. Storytelling and world identity

Before introducing a major saga such as The Cold Fire, viewers need to understand what Zeus World is all about. We need to establish:

- What Zeus World represents.
- Its origin and mythology.
- The significance of its different regions.
- Who lives there.
- What makes each location unique.
- How the community becomes part of the world.
- Why viewers should care about what happens within it.

The Cold Fire can remain a candidate for a future storyline, but it should not define or constrain the entire world.

There should be room for multiple simultaneous stories, mysteries, adventures and character-driven events.

### 5. Community integration

The real community should gradually become part of Zeus World.

However, this should not become a world that exists solely to react to TikTok gifting or battle events.

Community participation could eventually influence characters, events, discoveries, celebrations and the evolution of the world.

Recognition should not be exclusively monetary. Presence, participation, kindness, loyalty and meaningful contributions should also matter.

The fictional mythology of the world and the factual history of the community must remain clearly distinguishable.

### 6. Phased evolution

We do not need to build everything at once. We should establish the long-term vision and expand incrementally.

**Phase 1: Discover Zeus World**

- Establish the world's identity and mythology.
- Introduce ZEYA as the narrator.
- Narrated cinematic journeys.
- Location introductions.
- Introduce existing inhabitants.
- Manual and automatic exploration.
- Establish the initial storytelling experience.

**Phase 2: Bring the world to life**

- Named recurring characters.
- Character personalities and occupations.
- Daily routines.
- Environmental interactions.
- Contextual character animations.
- Scripted character conversations.
- ZEYA commentary and character introductions.

**Phase 3: Interactive living world**

- Dynamic character conversations.
- Character memory.
- Relationships between inhabitants.
- Autonomous activities.
- Emergent events.
- ZEYA interacting with characters.
- Zeus interacting with the world.
- Intelligent cinematic camera direction.

**Phase 4: Persistent stories**

- Continuing character storylines.
- Mysteries and discoveries.
- Persistent world state.
- Episodic adventures.
- Community-driven events.
- Recaps across LIVE sessions.
- Consequences of previous decisions and events.

**Phase 5: Expanding universe**

- New regions and settlements.
- New characters and cultures.
- Interactive adventures and quests.
- Audience participation.
- Community representation within the world.
- Larger cinematic productions.
- Multiple interconnected storylines.

### 7. Architectural consideration

While implementation will happen in phases, we should design the foundations with the eventual living-world experience in mind.

Avoid architectural decisions that permanently restrict Zeus World to a scripted cinematic tour.

The long-term architecture should accommodate persistent world state, character intelligence, memory, event orchestration, dialogue, narrative management and autonomous cinematic direction.

### Success criteria

The success of Zeus World should not be measured solely by how beautiful the environment looks. It should be measured by whether viewers:

- Become familiar with recurring characters.
- Develop favourite characters and locations.
- Look forward to ongoing conversations and stories.
- Remember events from previous LIVE sessions.
- Ask what happened while they were away.
- Become emotionally invested in the world.
- Return because they want to know what happens next.

Zeus World should eventually feel like an ongoing television series inside a living, open-world simulation, experienced live alongside Zeus and ZEYA.

For now, capture this as the long-term product vision, assess what already exists against it, and identify the incremental capabilities required. Do not begin implementation or asset generation yet. Let us discuss and finalize the direction first.

## Part 4. Foundations to lay from the first step

The owner asked that nothing built early should lock the world into a scripted tour. Seven choices keep the road open. All can be honoured in phase 1 at little cost.

1. **The world's state lives apart from the picture.** The page shows what the world says is happening; it does not decide it. (Constitution, principle 7.)
2. **One form for every scene.** A scene is: who, where, what they do, what is said, how the camera covers it. In phases 1 and 2 scenes are written by hand; in phase 3 they are written as they happen; the same player plays both. The present 19-shot journey becomes the first scene in this form, not a special case.
3. **Characters are records from the start.** Even eight of them: name, job, home, voice, personality, relationships, memories. Later intelligence reads and writes the same record.
4. **A clock, not only buttons.** Light and routines follow the world's time; the owner's buttons override it.
5. **The camera works from a list of "things worth watching".** Today the list is fixed. Later the director fills it.
6. **Two books, never mixed.** The **Myth** (fiction, written by us and approved by the owner) and the **Chronicle** (fact: only what real people really did). Zeya always makes plain which she is telling ("the old people say..." against "last Tuesday, this happened").
7. **Every spoken line is kept.** A line recorded once is never paid for twice, and the record of what was said is what recaps and memory are made from.

## Part 5. Cautions, and what needs the owner's ruling

### Cautions

- **Conversations worth hearing are the hard part, not the technology.** The owner has already named the risk: "meaningless AI-generated dialogue". The proposal is a writers' room first: scenes written and approved like the hero film scripts, so the cast's voices and humour are established by hand. Conversations written as they happen come only when there is a body of approved writing for them to live up to.
- **Faces do not move.** The characters have no lips or expressions. Conversations can be filmed like a stage play seen from the stalls: two people, body language, voices, captions. Close-ups of a speaking face would show the lack. The constitution already sets lip-sync aside as a project of its own.
- **Live writing has a running cost.** Recorded lines cost cents once. Dialogue written during a stream costs something every hour. That figure is not known and must be found before phase 3.
- **The stream machine.** OBS runs the world at 30 frames a second beside LIVE Studio, and the page's memory has never been measured. A cast, voices and a director all add load.

### Points where the constitution needs the owner's word

1. **Zeya and the myth.** The constitution says Zeya "never invents history" and narrates "only facts present in the world history". The vision asks her to tell mythology. Proposed reading: she may tell the Myth as written in the world bible, marked as legend, and may never invent beyond it; the rule against invention stands untouched for the community's Chronicle.
2. **A fictional cast beside real citizens.** The constitution pictured the inhabitants as the viewers themselves, with "AI townsfolk" filling the gaps. The vision puts written, recurring characters at the centre. Proposed: the cast are the townsfolk who were always here; community members are the newcomers who arrive and settle; a fictional character is never given a name that could be mistaken for a viewer's.
3. **Relationships.** The rule that Zeya never declares a relationship without consent is about real people. Proposed: the fictional cast may quarrel, befriend and fall out freely; a real person is never written into a relationship or a quarrel.
4. **A recorded Zeus.** The owner is Zeus, live. Proposed: no recorded Zeus voice in the world; Zeya speaks to him and he answers himself. The hero films keep their own narrator.
